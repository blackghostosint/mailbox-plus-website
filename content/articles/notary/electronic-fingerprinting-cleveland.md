## Not Your Fault: The Tuesday-Only Fingerprint Window

Name it the Capture-Fee Gap: the state's fee for the check itself is fixed, but the capture fee each WebCheck site charges on top is the site's to set — and they post it on the door. The sheriff window advertising $30 for the BCI check is the one locked at 3:30; the counter still open after your 6 p.m. shift posts $45 for the identical check, $58 for FBI-only, or $80 for both together. The spread is what you are buying: a live scanner, a person who reads the reason code with you before anything is submitted, and hours that match a working week. The closed $30 window cannot sell you that.

There are hundreds of WebCheck sites — the Attorney General's electronic fingerprint system — across the state. Most of them are shut while you are still on the clock: sheriff's offices with daytime hours, nonprofits open a few afternoons a week. The Attorney General's own listing tells you to contact a location before you visit to make sure its equipment is current — a maintenance reality the state felt the need to print at the top of its own directory. Add it up: the nearest location is often the least available one, and the errand competes with your job for the same hours.

Then there is the card rule. Some requesting agencies still accept — or require — rolled-ink prints on an FD-258 card, and ink is where rejections live. The fix is capturing the prints electronically first and printing them onto the card the agency wants — $35, no ink pad — instead of rolling your finger across a pad and hoping the loop survives the mail. It is the same digital capture behind [Ohio's digital fingerprinting background checks](/articles/ohio-digital-fingerprinting-background-checks/), just pointed at paper when the board insists on it.

And the clock is real: the Bureau of Criminal Investigation processes about 1.5 million background checks a year. When the state says a report decides whether you can start your job, a two-week scramble for a scanner slot is not a small inconvenience — it is the bottleneck between you and your paycheck.
