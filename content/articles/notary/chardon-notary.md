The notary public serves a vital function in our legal system, verifying identities and ensuring documents are signed willingly and authentically. It's a public trust, enshrined in law, with clear guidelines set by the Ohio Secretary of State notary regulations. Instead, what we often encounter is a system designed for the convenience of the provider, not the customer. Banks use notary services as a perk for their account holders, not a general public service. Libraries offer it as a goodwill gesture, constrained by volunteer availability. Retail shipping centers, while often charging a premium, prioritize their core shipping business, making notary services an often-secondary, unpredictable offering.

You, the individual Chardon resident with a life to live and a schedule to keep, are left to navigate this maze. You're expected to anticipate when a specific individual might be "in," whether they'll be "at lunch," or if you meet the arbitrary criteria to even _be_ served. It's an outdated model in a world that increasingly values efficiency and customer-centricity.
