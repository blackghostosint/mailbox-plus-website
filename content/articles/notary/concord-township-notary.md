## The Appointment Wall

Call the mechanism what it is: the Appointment Wall. Notary service that technically exists, but only behind a scheduling system, a membership requirement, or an employee who happens to be in the building.

Here's how the wall gets built, and it is not anyone's malice. In Ohio, a notary public can charge up to five dollars for a standard in-person notarial act — that is the statutory cap under Ohio Revised Code 147.08. Now put that number in front of a bank branch manager whose quarterly goals are measured in mortgage originations and credit-line openings. A five-dollar stamp is not a business line; it is a distraction from one. So the commission lands on whoever has spare capacity — an assistant manager, a platform banker, a teller who passed the state exam — people whose real jobs swallow their calendars whole.

The result is notarization by availability lottery. The sign in the window still says "Notary services available," because the commission is real and the branch is legally allowed to advertise it. But the service itself lives inside someone else's lunch schedule, vacation days, and loan appointments. Add the membership gates some credit unions run — you must bank there to get stamped there — and the Appointment Wall is complete: a legally simple act, fenced behind calendars and customer lists that have nothing to do with your document.

And the wall has a second face that costs people more than time. When you cannot find a notary today, the temptation is to sign the document at your kitchen table tonight and "just get it stamped later." Under Ohio law, that shortcut can void the whole thing. A notary must witness your signature in person or take your acknowledgment in person — and for a jurat, the notary must administer an oath or affirmation when you sign. Sign first, stamp later, and an honest notary has to turn you away, because stamping a backdated signature is exactly what the seal exists to prevent. The wall doesn't just waste your afternoon. It pressures you into mistakes that cost you the document, the filing deadline, sometimes the deal.
