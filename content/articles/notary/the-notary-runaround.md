# The Notary Runaround: One Signature Shouldn’t Take All Afternoon

![A clean clipboard with a contract ready for a fast, walk-in notary public seal at Mailbox Plus](articles/notary/the-notary-runaround-featured.webp)

The car title sat on the passenger seat.

It was ninety-two degrees in the shade, and the air conditioning in the sedan was taking its time. You had forty minutes left on your lunch break. The bank on Callow Road had a sign in the window that said _Notary Public_, but the teller behind the glass just shook her head.

"She’s out to lunch," the teller said. "Try the branch in Mentor."

You didn’t want to go to Mentor. You wanted to sign the paper, get the seal, and go back to work.

So you drove to the library. The clerk there looked at the clock, then at your form.

"Our notary left at two," she said.

That’s how the runaround works. It is quiet. It doesn't announce itself. It just eats your afternoon, one empty bank lobby and one apologetic shrug at a time.

## The Illusion of Availability

Most places that advertise [notary public services](/home-business/notary-services/) treat them like an afterthought. They have one person on the payroll who took the state exam. If that person is sick, or on vacation, or standing in line at the deli down the street, you are out of luck.

They don't care about your car title or your power of attorney. To them, it’s a two-dollar transaction that gets in the way of selling mortgages or checking out books.

You stand at the counter. You hold your pen. You wait for someone to find the one employee with the stamp.

It shouldn't be this hard to get a signature verified.
