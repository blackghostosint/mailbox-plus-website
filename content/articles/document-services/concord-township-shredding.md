## Not Your Fault: The Curb-Side Data Leak

The thing standing between you and a clean garage is not laziness. It is a mechanism that operates in plain daylight, and it has a name worth giving: the Curb-Side Data Leak.

Here is how it works. Every week, millions of households put documents with full account numbers, dates of birth, and Social Security numbers directly into trash bags and recycling bins — intact. The waste stream is not designed to protect you. There is no guard at the transfer station checking papers for sensitive information. Once that bin hits the curb, your financial history is physically accessible to anyone with five minutes and no shame. That is not paranoia; it is arithmetic. A single statement plus a piece of discarded junk mail gives a stranger your name, your address, your account number, and your bank. Identity theft built on dumpster-diving requires no hacking skill at all — the [FTC's identity theft guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/identity-theft) exists precisely because this crime is common enough to need a federal recovery infrastructure.

The second half of the mechanism is the home shredder industry. You probably own one. It cost forty dollars, it jams on staples, it overheats after ten pages, and it makes a noise like dying machinery. It was built for a three-page problem. You have a seven-year problem. The gap between those two things is where the boxes in your garage come from — and why they multiply instead of shrink.
