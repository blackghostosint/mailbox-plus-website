## The Home-Shredder Dead End, and Why It Keeps Happening

The thing standing between you and a clean closet is not laziness. It's a system that was never built for you, and it has a name: the Home-Shredder Dead End.

Here's how it runs. Every household with paper faces the same fork in the road. One path is the $40 desk shredder — rated for a handful of sheets, built for a three-page problem. It jams on staples, overheats after a few dozen pages, and leaves you with a bag of confetti strips you now have to trust to the trash. The other path is the commercial shredding service — pricing written for offices that purge by the pallet, with minimums and scheduling that make no sense for two boxes. You don't fit either instrument. That's the whole mechanism: the machine is too small, the contract is too big, and your two boxes fall through the gap. You weren't lazy. You were designed out.

And the risk is not theoretical. Identity theft from discarded paper needs no hacking skill at all — a statement plus a piece of junk mail gives a stranger your name, address, account number, and bank. It's common enough that the [FTC maintains a full federal recovery process for identity theft](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/identity-theft). The Dead End persists because the safe option was priced and packaged for somebody else. Not because you failed to buy the right appliance.
