## The Paper Avalanche

The box isn't your failure. The system that built the box is the problem, and it's worth naming: call it the Paper Avalanche.

Here's how it operates, in plain daylight. Every institution you deal with — the hospital, the county recorder, the bank, the IRS — still issues the most important documents of your life as a single paper copy, handed to you once. Then the storage burden is entirely yours. The hospital keeps its records digital and redundant. The county keeps its records digital and redundant. The one copy that exists for you exists on paper, in a cardboard box, indexed by your memory. Nobody hands you a backup of your deed at closing. Nobody emails you a second copy of your 2015 tax return.

The home scanner was supposed to be your escape hatch, and that's where the mechanism really closes the trap. A consumer flatbed is built for twenty pages, not a filing cabinet. You feed it a filing cabinet's worth of paper and it jams on staples, chokes on curled receipts, and asks you to name every file by hand. So you scan the first thirty pages with real determination, and then life happens, and the machine goes back in the closet next to the box it failed to eat. The equipment wasn't the problem. The volume was. A job that size is a production run, and production runs need a production line — not a Saturday and a USB cable.

You're not disorganized. You were handed two decades of single-copy paper records and a machine built for photo albums. Nobody wins that game.
