## Not Your Fault: The Fax Trap

Here's the strange part: you didn't fall behind. The technology you're being sent hunting for didn't survive because it's good. It survived because entire industries wired it into their rulebooks decades ago and never rewired.

Real estate is the classic case. A closing on a house near the Geauga County Courthouse can still hinge on a lender's faxed payoff letter, because somebody's intake process was built around a fax number in 1994 and nobody's job description includes rebuilding it. Healthcare runs on the same rails — an insurer whose appeal instructions still read "by mail or fax, please." Courts and county offices print the fax number right on the form, then leave the rest to you.

Ohio will let you sign a mortgage with a finger on a tablet. But the payoff letter travels by screeching phone line. The signature went digital; the delivery didn't.

And the trap operates in full legal daylight. The FCC keeps an entire [consumer FAQ on junk faxes](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/faqs-about-junk-faxes) — federal rules on consent and unsolicited faxes. A "dead" technology with its own page on a government website is not dead. It's infrastructure.

The trap hides in plain sight, too — in the "please fax" line of an intake form, in the fine print of a purchase agreement. You discover it at exactly the moment you can least afford a delay, which is precisely when the system collects best.

So when the requirement lands on your desk, understand what it actually is. The institution needs the fax. The institution hands you the burden. You're the last mile of somebody else's legacy workflow — and the last mile is always the part somebody makes you walk.
