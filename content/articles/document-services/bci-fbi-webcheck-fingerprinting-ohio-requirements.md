## The Reason Code Is the Whole Ballgame

The villain here is not a person. It is a blank field on a form.

Every Ohio background check runs under a reason code — an Ohio Revised Code number that tells BCI why your prints are being submitted. The Ohio Attorney General's FBI reason code table lists codes for specific licenses and jobs: 4723 09 for nurses, RNs, LPNs, and dialysis techs; 4735 143 for real estate salespersons and brokers; 3319 39 for public school employees and school bus drivers; 3319 291 for school licensure through the Ohio Department of Education; 2151 86 for out-of-home child care, foster parents, adoptive parents, and everyone 18 and over living in the home; 4757 101 for counselors, social workers, and marriage and family therapists; 4749 06 for private investigators and security guards. There are dozens more, and they change as legislation adds them.

That code decides everything downstream: which database your prints hit, whether the state sends a direct copy to your licensing board, and whether the submission is even valid. The Ohio Attorney General's office is blunt about what happens when it goes wrong: once a transaction is submitted, BCI cannot change the information. A new background check has to be submitted, and no credits are issued for submission errors.

Read that again. A wrong code is not a correction — it is a full second payment and a second wait, on the clock of a deadline your employer already gave you. (If you are still deciding where to get it done, our [electronic fingerprinting near Cleveland](/articles/electronic-fingerprinting-cleveland/) guide covers the location hunt; this one covers the paperwork.)

And the form does not tell you your code. The WebCheck request form has a blank line for the reason and blank lines for the BCI and FBI ORC numbers. The organization requesting the check is supposed to supply them. Plenty of packets do; some just say "get fingerprinted" and leave you to figure out the rest. If you show up without the code, or guess one, you are gambling your fee and your start date on a blank line.

## BCI vs FBI vs Both: The Comparison

The two checks are not competing products — they answer different questions.

**Ohio BCI check.** Searches Ohio criminal history records only. If your entire record history is in-state and your license is a state license, this may be all your board requires. Some Ohio licenses — a handful of the codes in the state's table — run BCI alone.

**FBI check.** Searches the national FBI database, covering records from all fifty states. It answers the question Ohio records cannot: what happened elsewhere? If you have lived out of state, boards that care about that fact will require it. And the FBI side only moves for specific Ohio employment purposes — it is not a walk-in service for personal curiosity.

**BCI and FBI together.** The most common pairing for licensing. Nurses, school employees, child care and foster/adoptive households, and many healthcare licenses require both, because the board wants the state record and the national one. On the WebCheck request form, that means an ORC number on both lines — and results that come back separately.

One detail that catches people: BCI and FBI results are processed separately. You can get a rejection letter on one while the other clears. Do not assume a rejection letter means everything failed, and do not assume silence on one side means both are fine — you resubmit only for the check that was rejected.
