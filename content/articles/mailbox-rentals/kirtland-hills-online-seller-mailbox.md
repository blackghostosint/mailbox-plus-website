Your street address prints on every label you ship — and you did not choose it. You sell from your home in Kirtland Hills. ## The Return-Label Printout

Call the mechanism what it is: the Return-Label Printout. Not a hacker, not a scam — a default setting, operating in plain daylight on millions of labels a day.

When you send an eBay return label, the process asks you to confirm your return address on the label before it goes to the buyer ([eBay's own seller guide](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/managing-returns-refunds/return-shipping-for-sellers?id=4703) walks you through exactly that step). When you print USPS labels from Etsy, the "Ship From" address on the label is the address saved in your shop settings — and Etsy's help pages tell you to edit that address in settings if it is wrong ([USPS Shipping Labels on Etsy](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000336887-USPS-Shipping-Labels-on-Etsy)). The marketplaces are not malicious. They simply assume the address you sell from is the address you want printed — and for a home seller, that assumption hands your front door to every buyer, every returns department, and every stranger who opens a box.

And a PO Box does not solve it — though credit where due: for plain letters, a PO Box does the job fine. The failure is packages. UPS states plainly in its own address guidelines that it cannot deliver to a PO Box ([UPS Address Guidelines](https://www.ups.com/worldshiphelp/WSA/ENG/AppHelp/mergedProjects/CORE/SHIPMENT/Country_Territory_Postal_Code_and_State_Prov_Guidelines_Popup.htm)), and neither can FedEx. So the moment a customer's carrier hands the box to UPS or FedEx — which is exactly how most marketplace returns move — the PO Box fails. The marketplaces leave you a false choice: print your home address, or use a box that cannot receive the packages.

## What It Actually Feels Like

It is the tape gun squeaking through the ninth box of the evening and the thought that arrives with it: how many labels have gone out this month? Every one of them a small announcement of where you sleep.

It is the doorbell ringing on a Tuesday afternoon and the half-second pause before you check the camera — neighbor, delivery, or a customer who decided a refund discussion is better conducted in person. It is the buyer with a $14 complaint and your full name and street on the label in their hand. It is glancing at the porch camera after a big shipment leaves, not because you did anything wrong, but because a hundred strangers now hold a map to your house.

None of it is dramatic. That is what makes it grind. It is a low hum of exposure under every order — and you have been absorbing it so long it feels like the cost of selling online. It is not. It is the cost of one address doing two jobs.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

Your home is where your family is. It is not a receiving dock, a returns department, or a public reception desk for your shop.

The system built for e-commerce assumed sellers have storefronts. Most do not. Kitchen-table sellers in Kirtland Hills and everywhere else got handed a default that treats the most private place in your life as a business address — and the platforms offer no simple, built-in way to separate the two unless you pay for a warehouse you do not need. Your home should not be printed on a label that travels to a stranger's mailbox. A business address should look like a business, and a home should look like home. There is no good reason those have to be the same line of text.

## What You Lose by Not Acting

**Privacy.** Every week you keep selling on your home address is another batch of labels in strangers' hands. The printout does not expire — a buyer from last spring still has your street on an old label, and data brokers collect what shipping records expose.

**Optionality.** A PO Box handles letters, but it cannot receive UPS or FedEx — that is carrier policy, not our opinion — so it quietly fails the moment a return moves on a private carrier. Staying boxed in means every carrier-delivered package either goes to your porch when you are not home or gets rerouted into another errand.
