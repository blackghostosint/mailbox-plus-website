## The Price You See Is Not the Price You Pay

You're comparing mailbox costs tonight — tabs open, coffee gone cold, and every website telling you a different number. One says "$9.99 a month." Another says "$25." The post office says "$21 for three months." What does a mailbox actually cost?

Here's the honest answer: **the advertised price is almost never the price you pay.** Every mailbox option hides part of its real cost in fees, size limits, and contracts that only show up after you've signed. This article is the straight version — what each option costs per month, what's included, and what quietly isn't.

We're Mailbox Plus, a local mailbox store in Concord Township, Ohio. We rent private mailboxes for a living, so we know exactly how the comparison works — including the ways our own option can disappoint if it's not what you need. No marketing spin. Just the numbers.

## The Four Ways to Rent a Mailbox

There are really only four mailbox products in the US, and every company selling them is selling one of these four:

1. **A PO Box** — a numbered compartment at the post office, rented from USPS.
2. **A store mailbox** — a mailbox at a private store like The UPS Store.
3. **A virtual mailbox** — a mailbox at a local store, managed remotely through a national middleman like iPostal1 or Anytime Mailbox.
4. **A private mailbox (PMB)** — a mailbox rented directly from a local mailbox store (a Commercial Mail Receiving Agency, or CMRA).

The names change, but the products don't. Here's what each one actually costs.

## Option 1: The PO Box — Cheapest Sticker, Smallest Box

The post office rents five PO Box sizes, officially named Size 1 through Size 5 (XS to XL). The USPS publishes the dimensions: Size 1 is 3" x 5.5", Size 2 is 5" x 5.5", Size 3 is 5.5" x 11", Size 4 is 11" x 11", and Size 5 is 12" x 22.5". Most boxes are about 14.75 inches deep.

**PO Box pricing depends on two things: the size and the location.** USPS groups post offices into fee tiers, and the same box can cost very different amounts across town. The official price list (USPS Notice 123) shows the smallest boxes starting around $21 per quarter at cheaper locations — roughly $7 a month. Larger boxes at expensive locations run over $100 a month. A realistic mid-range small box runs about $15–$25 a month.

Here's what the sticker price doesn't tell you:

- **Only USPS delivers to a PO Box.** FedEx, UPS, and DHL drivers do not deliver to post office boxes. Every package from those carriers becomes a trip to a carrier facility, a redirected delivery, or a missed shipment.
- **Size is a hard ceiling.** Size 1 holds roughly 10–15 letters. If you receive packages — and most mailbox renters do — a PO Box is too small before you start.
- **You pay by the quarter or the year.** If you cancel early, you've paid for time you don't use.

The PO Box is the classic "cheap until it isn't" option. For a person who receives only letters, it works. For anyone with packages, the real cost shows up as missed deliveries and wasted trips.

## Option 2: The Store Mailbox — Franchise Fees and Fine Print

The UPS Store and similar franchise locations rent real street-address mailboxes. The UPS Store's own site says the price is set by each independently owned location: "prices may vary. Contact your neighborhood location for pricing." That's the first honest thing you'll hear from them, and it's a warning.

**What you pay:** Depending on the store, small mailboxes commonly run about $15–$30 a month, with larger boxes higher. But the monthly fee is only part of it:

- **Activation fees.** Many stores charge a one-time setup fee on top of the first month.
- **Contract terms.** Six-month and twelve-month agreements are common. The UPS Store's mailbox agreement template shows monthly forwarding fees billed in advance — you're committing to the term.
- **Forwarding and holding fees.** If you travel and want your mail forwarded, that's billed separately and in advance.
- **Carrier coverage varies by store.** The UPS Store's national FAQ says mailbox holders can receive packages from any carrier, but coverage is set store-by-store — some locations refuse FedEx and DHL drop-offs because they're competitors. You must ask your specific store.

A store mailbox isn't a bad product. It's just a product with variable pricing and a contract — two things that make the "monthly cost" hard to compare. When a price has to be quoted individually, you're not comparing prices; you're negotiating.

## Option 3: The Virtual Mailbox — The $9.99 That Isn't $9.99

This is the trap that catches the most people. National services like iPostal1 and Anytime Mailbox advertise plans starting around $9.99–$15 a month. That price sounds unbeatable — until you read the fee schedule.

Here's what iPostal1's own pricing page lists (and these are typical of the category):

- **Plan price:** $9.99–$39.99 a month, and the cheapest plans cap how many mail items you get — 30 items a month on the entry plan.
- **Over-limit fees:** $0.35 per mail item above your plan's cap.
- **Content scanning:** $2.25 per envelope to open and scan up to 10 pages, then $0.25 per page after that. Many customers want a scan of every important envelope — that's $2.25 each.
- **Extended storage:** $1.10 per mail item per month after the free storage window ends.
- **Check deposit:** $4.95 to deposit up to five checks, plus scan and shipping costs.
- **Forwarding:** shipping costs on every package you forward, plus consolidation fees around $1.95 per package.

Add it up for a normal month: base plan plus scans on ten envelopes, a couple of packages forwarded, a check deposited, and a few items stored — and the "cheap" virtual mailbox is now $40–$60 a month. **The per-item fees are the product.** The low base price is the advertisement.

There's a second cost that never shows on a bill: the middleman markup. iPostal1 and Anytime Mailbox don't own the stores where your mail lands. They rent space at local mail centers — places exactly like ours — and sell you remote access to a mailbox a local store could have rented to you directly. You're paying a national call center to manage a mailbox at a shop down the road.

## What It Costs You to Pick Wrong

Choosing on the sticker price has a way of billing you later:

- **The PO Box that can't take packages** means a FedEx "missed delivery" slip every time something important ships — and a trip to a facility at the other end of town during their hours, not yours.
- **The virtual mailbox with per-item fees** turns every envelope into a decision: pay $2.25 to see what's inside, or let it sit and pay $1.10 a month in storage.
- **The store mailbox with a contract** locks you in for six to twelve months — and if you move or the store's service disappoints, you're paying for a box you're not using.

None of these are scams. They're all real products that work for the right person. They just cost more than the ad says, and the difference is almost never disclosed on the pricing page.
