# Your Hambden LLC Filing Has Your Home Address in the Public Record

![A small business storefront window in rural Geauga County with a hand-painted sign](articles/mailbox-rentals/hambden-small-business-mailbox-featured.webp)

You started a small construction company in Hambden. You run it from your house on Spruce Lane. You filed the LLC paperwork with the Ohio Secretary of State online. It took fifteen minutes.

You put your home address on the filing. The form asked for it. You had no other address to use. You clicked submit.

Now your home address is on the public record.

## What Public Means

Anyone can search Ohio business records. Your name. Your home address. Your spouse's name if it is a joint filing. The street where your kids ride the bus.

This is not a data breach. This is not a hack. This is the legal requirement for doing business in Ohio. The state publishes your home address so creditors and process servers can find you. They can find you. So can your competitors. So can marketing lists. So can anyone with an internet connection and five minutes.

The guy down the street who does the same kind of work — he can look you up. He knows where you live. He knows where you run your business. He knows where your pickup truck is parked at night.

## The Address Problem

Your Hambden home is a house. It is not a commercial zone. It is not a place where clients should show up unannounced. But the public record points there.

When a supplier sends invoices, they come to your home. When a customer wants to file a complaint, the address in the court filing is your home. When a web scraper collects Ohio business data for a lead generation list, your home address is in it.

You cannot use a PO box for your LLC. The Ohio Secretary of State requires a physical street address for the principal place of business. A PO box does not count.

A real street address at a commercial location does.
