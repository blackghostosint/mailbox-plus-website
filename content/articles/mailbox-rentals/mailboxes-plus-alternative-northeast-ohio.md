## You Typed a Name and Got a Dozen Different Stores

You searched for "Mailboxes Plus" because you need a mailbox — a real one, at a real address, where a person takes your packages instead of leaving them on a porch. And the search results gave you a maze: a Mailboxes Plus in Kentucky, one in California, one in Rhode Island, none of them near you, none of them the same business, all of them sharing a name that belongs to no single national chain.

But the maze is not the real trap. Here is the rule, printed in USPS guidance and nowhere near the rental agreement: a Post Office box takes only Postal Service mail. UPS cannot put a package in one. FedEx cannot. Amazon cannot. The post office does not have to warn you when you rent the box, and you find out the first time a tracking page says "delivery attempted." If you live anywhere in Lake County — Concord Township, Mentor, Painesville, Willoughby, Kirtland — the fix is close: Mailbox Plus at 7554 Fredle Drive in Concord Township, just off Crile Road, minutes from SR-44 and I-90, rents private mailboxes at a street address every carrier can deliver to.

## The Part Nobody Warns You About

You squint at your phone, thumb past a Mailboxes Plus in Kentucky, then one in California, then Rhode Island — and Concord Township only shows up after you add "Ohio" to the search. The name you typed is not a brand with a locator map; independent shipping stores all over the country picked it on their own.

That confusion hides a bigger problem. The mailbox that looks cheapest in the search results is legally barred from doing the one job you are renting it for. USPS's own guidance says private carriers like UPS, FedEx, and Amazon cannot place a package in a PO Box. So the box rents fine, the address prints fine, and then the parcels you actually order — the ones that arrive by ground truck, not by postal carrier — bounce.

Nobody at the post office counter is obligated to tell you that before you pay. You find out standing in your driveway, holding your phone, reading "delivery attempted — no access to delivery location" while a customer message waits unanswered asking where their order is. That is the moment your business stops looking real to the person who paid you.

## Why This Quietly Stopped Making Sense

It should not take a research project to rent a mailbox. The setup itself is simple and standardized: USPS requires Form 1583 — the application for delivery of mail through an agent — plus two forms of identification, one with a photo. That form is the whole legal machinery. It authorizes the store to receive your mail, and it protects you, because the store cannot hand your mail to anyone who has not been authorized on that form.

What is not standardized is everything around the form. Whether someone at the counter knows your face when you walk in. Whether the store accepts the UPS and FedEx packages that a PO Box legally cannot hold. Whether you can call the store and ask "did anything come for me today?" and get a human answer. Those are the things that actually decide whether a mailbox rental is worth paying for — and none of them show up in a search result for a shared store name.
