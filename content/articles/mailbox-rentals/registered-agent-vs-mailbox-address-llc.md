## The Two Addresses on Every LLC Checklist

You're forming your LLC and the checklist keeps asking for two things: a **registered agent** and a **business address**. One form says "street address required." Another says "no PO boxes." A third service you're considering says "we'll be your registered agent — oh, and you'll also need a physical address for your LLC."

It's confusing because everyone talks about them as if they're the same thing. They are not. They are two separate jobs, filled by two separate addresses, and — this is the part nobody explains — the LLC needs **both**.

This article clears it up: what a registered agent actually does, what a business address actually does, what Ohio requires for each, and the one thing a private mailbox **cannot** do for your LLC.

## What a Registered Agent (Statutory Agent) Actually Is

In Ohio, the registered agent is called the **statutory agent**, and it's a legal role, not a mailing preference. Under Ohio Revised Code Section 1706.09, every Ohio LLC and every out-of-state LLC doing business in Ohio "must appoint and maintain a statutory agent to accept service of process."

Service of process is the legal phrase for being handed official documents: a lawsuit summons, a subpoena, a court notice, or certain official state notices. When someone sues your LLC, the court doesn't hunt for you on Facebook — it serves your statutory agent at the address on file with the Secretary of State. That's the entire job: **someone, at a specific Ohio address, who can be handed legal documents and who will get them to you.**

Because that job matters, Ohio sets strict rules for who and what qualifies. From the Ohio Secretary of State's own instructions on Form 610, the Articles of Organization:

- The statutory agent must be a **natural person residing in Ohio**, or a domestic or foreign **business entity with an Ohio address**.
- The agent's address must be a **primary residence** or a **usual place of business** — and it must be an **Ohio** address.
- **Post Office boxes are NOT allowed** (the one exception: if both a PO Box and a Rural Route number are provided).
- **Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA) addresses are NOT allowed.** The form defines a CMRA as "a private business that rents private mailboxes to customers."

Read that last line twice, because it's the part most mailbox-services marketing won't tell you: **your private mailbox cannot be your LLC's registered agent address in Ohio.** A PMB is a street address for receiving mail — but the statutory agent slot requires a place where a person is present during normal hours to accept legal documents, and Ohio explicitly excludes commercial mailbox agencies from that role.

The agent also has to sign an Acceptance of Appointment, and the agent's name and address go **on the public record** — anyone can look them up in the Ohio business search. Miss this and the consequences aren't subtle: Ohio can cancel your entity for failing to maintain a statutory agent.

## What a Business Address Actually Is

The business address is the address your LLC lives at in the everyday world. It's the address that goes on:

- Your **business bank account** (banks must collect a street address under the federal Customer Identification Program — a PO box alone won't pass)
- Your **business licenses and permits**
- Your **vendor and supplier forms** (which routinely say "physical address required — no PO box")
- Your **website, invoices, and business cards**
- Your **shipping labels and return addresses**
- The contact information on your LLC's public filings beyond the agent slot

This is a different job from the statutory agent. Nobody is handing a lawsuit to your business address; they're sending invoices, bank statements, tax documents, supplier shipments, and customer mail. The business address is where your LLC **runs**, not where it gets **served**.

## Both Are Needed. Here's the Split.

Think of it as two doors on the same building:

|                                  | Statutory Agent                                             | Business Address                                         |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Job**                          | Accept legal service of process                             | Receive mail, packages, filings, statements              |
| **Ohio requirement**             | Required by O.R.C. 1706.09                                  | Required in practice by banks, licenses, vendors         |
| **Address rule**                 | Ohio residence or place of business; **no PO box, no CMRA** | Street address; PO boxes often rejected by banks/vendors |
| **On the public record?**        | Yes — agent name + address                                  | Your choice of what you list                             |
| **Can a private mailbox do it?** | **No** — Ohio excludes CMRAs                                | **Yes** — a PMB is a real street address                 |

The mistake most new LLC owners make is trying to use one address for both jobs. If you use your home for everything, your home goes on the public record and into every vendor database. If you use a private mailbox for everything, Ohio will reject your filing — because the statutory agent slot can't be a mailbox. Neither extreme works.

## What Ohio Actually Requires (Straight From the Form)

Here's the honest, verified breakdown of Form 610, so you're not guessing:

1. **Name your LLC.** It must include a designator like "LLC" or "Limited Liability Company."
2. **Appoint a statutory agent.** A natural person residing in Ohio, or a business entity with an Ohio address, who agrees to accept service of process. The agent signs an Acceptance of Appointment.
3. **Give the agent's Ohio street address.** Primary residence or usual place of business. No PO box. No CMRA. No exceptions for mailbox suites.
4. **File it.** The state filing fee is $99, and the form must be typed.

That's it for the filing itself. The business address — the one for your bank, your licenses, your vendors, your website — is not a field on the state form, but every institution you touch afterward will ask for it, and most will reject a PO box.

## What That Means for You, Step by Step

**Step 1 — Fill the statutory agent slot.** You have three legitimate options: use your own home address (legal, but it goes on the public record); use a trusted Ohio resident's address with their consent (same public-record caveat); or hire a commercial registered-agent service — a real Ohio office that accepts service of process and forwards documents to you. The last option typically costs $50–$150 a year and keeps your name and address off the public agent slot entirely. Ask your accountant or attorney what they recommend for your situation; this is the one slot a mailbox can't fill.

**Step 2 — Give your LLC a business address that isn't your home.** This is where a **private mailbox** earns its keep. A PMB at a licensed mailbox service is a real street address — your own suite number at a real commercial location — that all four carriers (USPS, UPS, FedEx, DHL) deliver to. It's not a PO box. It satisfies the "physical address required" fields on bank accounts, vendor forms, licenses, and your website. And it keeps your home address out of every database your business touches.

**Step 3 — Keep the two separate and document both.** Your statutory agent is the address the courthouse uses; your business address is the address the world uses. Write both down, keep the agent's contact current (Ohio cancels entities that lose their agent), and use your business address everywhere your LLC operates.

## The Cost of Getting It Wrong

Skipping either half isn't free — the cost just shows up later.

- **No statutory agent (or a rejected one):** Ohio won't accept your filing, or will cancel your entity for failing to maintain an agent. Your LLC doesn't exist until it's filed.
- **Missed legal documents:** If a lawsuit summons goes to an address nobody checks, you can lose by default judgment without ever seeing the paperwork.
- **Home address everywhere:** Every bank, vendor, and license adds your home to another database — and those lists get bought, merged, and leaked.
- **A PO box that fails the form:** You'll be typing your home address into the street-address fields anyway, because the PO box got rejected.
