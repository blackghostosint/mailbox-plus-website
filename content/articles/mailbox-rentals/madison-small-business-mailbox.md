## The Public Record Default

Call it what it is: **The Public Record Default**.

When you form an Ohio LLC, the state requires you to appoint and maintain a statutory agent — the person or business that receives legal documents and official notices — and that agent's name and street address go on the filing with the Ohio Secretary of State. It is searchable. Anyone can look it up, for free, in under a minute.

For most small business owners, that address is the only address the business has: the house. The agent is you, the address is your home, and the record says so forever, because the Secretary of State lists the statutory agent name and address on its public business search.

It does not stop there. Bank forms, vendor applications, business cards, invoices, insurance paperwork — every one of them asks for a business address, and every one of them quietly becomes another copy of your home address floating around in someone else's filing cabinet.

None of it is a scam. All of it is a default. And defaults don't ask you whether you'd rather keep your front door out of it.

## The Honest Comparison: PO Box vs Street-Address Mailbox

We'll show you the whole board, because a PO Box is the right tool for some jobs:

- **Carrier reach.** UPS cannot deliver to a PO Box — its own address guidelines warn that a shipment addressed to a PO Box experiences delays, loses service guarantees, and is assessed an address-correction surcharge. FedEx says the only domestic PO Box delivery option is FedEx Ground Economy; its primary services don't reach a box. DHL's answer is flat: shipments cannot be delivered to PO boxes. USPS does deliver to PO Boxes — that's their home turf. A street-address mailbox at our counter takes deliveries from all four.
- **Form-friendliness.** Here's the one that matters for an LLC specifically: Ohio's own statutory agent update form states in writing that post office boxes and CMRAs are not allowed as the agent address — the agent address must be a real Ohio street address where the agent can be reached. And some banks and payment processors flag PO Boxes during verification. A street-address mailbox clears both hurdles; a PO Box fails the first one outright.
- **USPS street addressing.** USPS does offer a Premium PO Box street-addressing option at participating post offices, which lets some private carriers reach a box. Availability varies by post office, and it still comes with restrictions — check with your local office before relying on it.
- **Reception and handling.** At our counter, packages are signed for and held behind the counter — not left at an empty commercial lobby or on a porch.

So: if your business only receives envelopes, a PO Box can work. The moment suppliers ship you UPS Ground and the Secretary of State's form rules out a box, a street address is the tool.
