## The Address On Every Box You Ship

Look at the last label you printed. Not the tracking number — the return address. That is your house. Your street number, your street name, your town. It went out with every package you shipped last week, and it will go out with every package this week.

Now ask yourself the question sellers on r/Etsy and r/Flipping have been asking for years: who is looking at it?

A buyer with a grudge. A stranger who wants to know where their package came from — and how close that is to them. Someone who reverse-searches an address the way people reverse-search phone numbers. The r/Etsy threads about buyers who "looked them up" are real, and they never end with "and it turned out fine."

Your home is your business's shipping department. That is the problem. And it is a fixable one — but the fix has gotten more urgent, and more specific, because the platforms changed the rules.

## eBay Now Displays Seller Addresses (Thanks to a Federal Law)

Here is what changed, and it wasn't a rumor.

In 2023, the **INFORM Consumers Act** went into effect — a federal law that requires online marketplaces to collect, verify, and disclose information about high-volume sellers. On eBay, the mechanics are spelled out in plain language in the Seller Center:

- If you sell 200 or more new or unused items totaling more than $5,000 in a 12-month period, eBay must collect and verify your bank account, tax ID, phone, and email.
- Once you reach **$20,000 in annual revenue on eBay**, the platform is required to include your name (or company name) **and your full physical address** in purchase confirmation emails and on the buyer's order details page.

Not a PO box number and a zip code. Your full physical address — the one eBay has on file as your registered address.

There are exceptions, and they matter:

- **If your registered address is residential**, eBay may show only your state and country instead of the full address — but you have to request the exception, and qualify for it.
- **If you use a different address for product returns**, that return address is what gets displayed.

Read that second one again, because it is the whole game: **the address buyers see is the address you give the platform.** If your registered address and your return address are your home, then your home is what gets disclosed — on every order confirmation, to every buyer, at $20K of sales.

A seller on the eBay community boards put it bluntly: once you cross that threshold, your address is no longer private information. It's part of the transaction.

## Etsy Doesn't Show Your Address — Your Labels Do

Etsy is different, and the difference is sneakier.

Etsy itself does not publish your address to buyers the way eBay's order details page does — sellers report that Etsy gives buyers an email, not a street address. But look at what actually happens when a sale ships: you print the label. Etsy's label tool pulls your return address. The buyer, or the buyer's mail carrier, or anyone else who handles that package sees your home address on the box.

That's why the r/Etsy "safety aspect" threads go the way they do. One seller with 1,800+ sales says it's "never been an issue." Another seller asks how to keep buyers from finding their home, and the answers are a graveyard of workarounds: use a fake address (don't — it violates carrier rules), use a PO box (fine for USPS, but it doesn't help you on Amazon), rent a box at a UPS store (expensive).

The clean answer — a real street address that isn't your house — rarely shows up in those threads. That's the gap this article is here to close.

## Amazon Needs a Verifiable Address — and It Rejects PO Boxes

Amazon is the strictest of the three, and its rule is the least forgiving.

Amazon verifies the business address on your Seller Central account, and it does not accept PO boxes. Seller after seller hits the same wall: Amazon wants "a real street address — not a P.O. Box," verifiable through documents like a utility bill, bank statement, or lease agreement, and sometimes confirmed with a postcard code sent to that address.

A PO box fails that test. A home address passes it — but then you're right back to the first problem: your home is now the address on file with Amazon, visible in your seller information and connected to your business.

What sellers actually need is a street address that isn't a PO box **and** isn't home. That's the exact definition of a private mailbox.

## What It Costs to Keep Your Home on the Label

Let's be concrete about the other option — doing nothing.

- **At $20K in eBay sales,** your full home address goes into every order confirmation. That's not speculation; it's the law, stated in eBay's own INFORM Act guidance.
- **Your address is now searchable.** Reverse-address tools, property records, and social search connect a street address to a name in minutes. The r/Etsy sellers who worry about this aren't paranoid; they're early.
- **A PO box doesn't fix Amazon.** It's the wrong tool for the strictest platform, which leaves you running a two-address system that leaks anyway.
- **Porch theft gets personal.** When a package goes missing, the thief now knows exactly where the seller lives. Our [package protection article](/articles/porch-theft-lake-county-protect-packages/) covers what that actually looks like in Lake County.

None of these are dramatic on day one. They're the cost of a slow leak — until the day one of them isn't slow.
