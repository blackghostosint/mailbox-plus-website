## The Home-Address Label

Call the villain what it is: **The Home-Address Label**.

It's not a hacker and not a scam. It's the default. You open a seller account, you type in your address, and from that moment the platform stamps it onto every shipping label, every return label, every packing slip, every carrier manifest. Hundreds of labels over a year, all pointing back to your kitchen table.

The platforms don't hide it — the label generator simply uses the address on file, and for a home-based seller the address on file is your house. Seller help pages at major marketplaces walk you through fixing a wrong return address on labels, which tells you how central the return address is to the whole flow: it's on the label by design. Dig through the settings and you'll find most marketplaces still expect a physical return address for every listing.

USPS builds its own layer on top. Under the Postal Service's Domestic Mail Manual, a return address is required on most mail, and specific rules cover Priority Mail, insured mail, and anything carrying an ancillary service endorsement — in practice, most of what an online seller ships. The rule exists so undeliverable pieces can come back to you. The side effect is that the sender's address sits on the outside of nearly every box you send, readable by anyone who handles it.

Now think about who reads that corner of the box. Your buyer. Their family. Their neighbor who signs for the package. The carrier driver. Anyone the box gets misdelivered to. The Federal Trade Commission's identity-theft guidance treats personal details that slip into the wrong hands as the raw material thieves work with — and a home address paired with a name is one of the simplest pieces of that material to collect. It arrives gift-wrapped, in person, every time you ship.

The mechanism is legal, ordinary, and almost never questioned. That's what makes it work so well.

## Why a Street Address Beats a PO Box for a Seller

Here's the honest comparison, because you deserve the whole board:

- **Carrier coverage.** UPS cannot deliver to a PO Box — its own address guidelines say a shipment addressed to a PO Box can be delayed, loses service guarantees, and gets hit with an address-correction surcharge. FedEx's shipping FAQ says the only domestic PO Box delivery option is FedEx Ground Economy, a contract-only service; its main services don't reach a box. DHL's answer to "can I ship to a PO Box?" is flat: shipments cannot be delivered to PO boxes. USPS delivers to PO Boxes. A street-address mailbox at our counter takes all four.
- **Marketplace and bank forms.** Some marketplaces, banks, and payment processors flag a PO Box during address verification, and policies vary — a street address clears those forms without a fight.
- **Packages.** A PO Box holds letters and USPS flat pieces. Some post offices offer a street-addressing option that lets a box take carrier packages where available, but availability varies and the address is still a post-office address, not your own street. A street-address mailbox at our counter takes every carrier as a matter of course — signed for, held behind the counter, not on your porch.

So if you only ever receive envelopes, a PO Box is a fine tool. The moment you sell physical goods, it's the wrong tool.
