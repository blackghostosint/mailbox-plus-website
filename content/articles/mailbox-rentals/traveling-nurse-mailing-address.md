## The 13-Week Problem Nobody Warns You About

You signed a 13-week contract in a new city. The apartment is furnished, the orientation is scheduled, and for the first time in weeks you let yourself exhale.

Then the question hits: _where is my mail going to go?_

Your bank statement, your license renewal notice, your W-2 from the agency, the replacement debit card your bank is mailing — none of them know you moved to a temporary address. They know the address on file, and right now that address is either your old apartment, your parents' house, or a mailbox you are not sure still has your name on it.

You are not alone in this. Every travel nurse eventually asks the same three questions:

1. **Where do I receive mail during a 13-week contract?**
2. **What happens to my license renewal and tax documents?**
3. **How do I get packages without trusting a temp-housing front desk?**

The answer that keeps working — assignment after assignment — is a stable, real street address that does not change when you do. Here is how travel nurses make that happen, and why a private mailbox at a local store beats every other option you will find on a travel nurse forum.

## What Travel Nurses Actually Deal With

Let's be honest about the mail side of travel nursing, because nobody prepares you for it.

**Temp housing does not want your mail.** Extended-stay hotels, corporate apartments, and short-term rentals are set up for guests, not residents. Front desks lose packages, hold mail in a pile, or refuse it entirely. Landlords renting to traveling nurses have literally asked in online groups whether they are required to accept mail for tenants. If you are lucky, your housing has a locker. If you are not, your packages get returned to sender while you are on a 12-hour shift.

**License renewal mail is not optional.** You cannot practice without a current license. If your license is in Ohio, the Ohio Board of Nursing requires you to keep a current address of record and to report an address change within 30 days — and Ohio renewal happens online through eLicense, which means a current, reliable address is how you stay in the loop. Miss the window because a renewal notice went to an address you left six months ago, and you are off orientation, off the floor, and off payroll while it gets sorted.

**The tax-home question is real.** Travel nurses collect tax-free stipends because they maintain a tax home — a permanent address they return to and intend to come back to. If the IRS cannot see a real, consistent address that anchors your tax home, the stipend treatment gets complicated fast. This is not something to guess about; it is something to keep clean on paper with a stable address and a tax professional who knows travel nursing.

**Everything important is paper.** W-2s, health insurance cards, jury duty notices, voter registration, DMV documents, a credit card replacement after a fraud alert — the pieces of an adult life do not pause while you are between contracts. If that paperwork has nowhere to land, you will spend your days off chasing it.

## Why a PO Box Is Not the Answer

The first thing nurses try is a PO Box at the post office, and it works — for letters. Then the FedEx driver shows up with a package and you find out the hard way:

- **PO Boxes only accept USPS mail.** FedEx, UPS, and DHL will not deliver to a PO Box number. The USPS Street Addressing program can let private carriers deliver to some post offices, but it is subject to availability and restrictions at each location.
- **Big packages never fit.** Anything larger than the box requires counter pickup during business hours — or a secure parcel locker, when one exists.
- **A number is not an address.** Banks, employers, and the IRS ask for a physical address. A "PO Box 123" on a tax-home document raises questions a street address never will.

None of that is a dig at USPS. It is simply the wrong tool for a job that requires a real street address that accepts every carrier.
