## The Home-Address Default

The villain here has a boring name: the home-address default. Online marketplaces, suppliers, and shipping platforms all assume the person shipping from a kitchen table ships _from home_. So the return label they generate shows your residence. The supplier's packing slip shows your residence. The customer who wants a refund can read your residence right off the box.

This is not a hack or a leak. It is the system working exactly as designed — for warehouses and storefronts, not for the fastest-growing kind of seller there is: the one working out of a spare room in Willowick. The platforms treat your front door as your receiving dock. They have never asked whether you agreed to that trade.

The scale of the exposure is what gets people. One sale, one label, no problem. But an active seller puts out dozens or hundreds of labeled packages a year, many of them with a return policy that routes straight back to your house. Your address stops being something you share and becomes something you _publish_.

## What It Actually Feels Like

You know the moment. The doorbell rings during dinner, and your stomach drops before you even check the camera — is it a neighbor, or is it a customer? Someone messages a marketplace at 10 PM asking to "just swing by and return it in person," and you spend twenty minutes composing a reply that doesn't give away your schedule.

You start making small, quiet adjustments. You stop listing your city on your shop profile, even though buyers like local sellers. You hesitate before posting the photos from your workspace because the window shows the street. You consider a PO Box, then remember your marketplace needs a street address for carriers that will not deliver to a box — and the anxiety settles right back in.

None of this is the work you got into business to do. You wanted to source well, pack well, and keep customers happy. Instead, part of your brain is permanently assigned to managing the risk that your own address created.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

A residence and a business address are two different tools for two different jobs. One protects the people you love. The other receives commerce. The fact that thousands of sellers across Northeast Ohio have to blur those two jobs together — not because they chose to, but because the default shipped that way — is backwards.

Running a business from home should be the _smart_ arrangement, not the risky one. The infrastructure to separate the two already exists and costs about the same as a streaming subscription or two. The only reason sellers keep shipping their home address around Lake County is that nobody told them the separation takes fifteen minutes to set up.
