## The Return Address That Ships Itself

The problem has no name on any invoice, so give it one: **the home-address label**.

It's not a hack and it's not a scam. It's the default. You open a seller account, the form asks for an address, and you type in the only one you have. From that moment the platform stamps it onto every shipping label, every return label, every packing slip, and every carrier manifest — hundreds of labels a year, all pointing back to your kitchen table.

The platforms don't hide it, because for them it isn't a problem. The label generator simply uses the address on file, and for a home seller the address on file is your house. Marketplace help pages walk sellers through correcting a wrong return address on labels, which tells you how central that corner of the label is to the whole machine: it's there by design, on every shipment.

Here's the ugly part: even the Postal Service forces your home onto the box. Its own rulebook requires a return address on most mail, with stricter rules for Priority, insured, and endorsed pieces — most of what you ship. The rule exists so undeliverable packages can come back to you, but the side effect is your address sitting on the outside of nearly every box, readable by anyone who handles it.

Now count who reads that corner. Your buyer. Their roommate who signs for it. The delivery driver. Whoever's porch it lands on by mistake. The Federal Trade Commission's identity-theft guidance treats personal details that slip into the wrong hands as the raw material fraud works with — and a name paired with a home address is the simplest piece of that material to collect. It arrives gift-wrapped, in person, every time you ship.

The mechanism is legal, ordinary, and almost never questioned. That's exactly what makes it work so well.

## Why a Street Address Beats a PO Box for a Seller

Here's the honest comparison, because you deserve the whole board:

- **Carrier coverage.** UPS's own address guidelines say a shipment addressed to a PO Box can be delayed, loses its service guarantee, and gets hit with an address-correction surcharge. FedEx's shipping FAQ says the only domestic PO Box delivery option is FedEx Ground Economy, a contract-only service — its main services don't reach a box. DHL's answer on shipping to a PO Box is flat: no. USPS delivers to PO Boxes. A street-address mailbox at our counter takes all four.
- **Marketplace and bank forms.** Some marketplaces, banks, and payment processors flag a PO Box during address verification, and the policies vary by platform — a street address clears those forms without a fight.
- **Packages.** A PO Box holds letters and USPS flat pieces. Some post offices offer a street-addressing option that lets a box take packages from other carriers, but it's a premium add-on, subject to availability, and the address is still the post office's street — not your own. A street-address mailbox at our counter takes every carrier as a matter of course: signed for, held behind the counter, not on your driveway.

So if you only ever receive envelopes, a PO Box is a fine tool. The moment you sell physical goods, it's the wrong tool.
