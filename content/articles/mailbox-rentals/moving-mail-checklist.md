## The Part of Moving Nobody Checklists

You packed the boxes. You booked the truck. You changed the utilities. You even remembered to forward your mail — or so you think.

Then the first week in the new place, the surprises start. A bill you never saw goes to collections. The DMV says your registration renewal was "returned to sender." A package from UPS sits on the old porch for three days. And your favorite magazine just... stops coming, even though you paid for two more years.

Here is what almost nobody tells you about moving: **a USPS change of address does not change your address.** Not really. It reroutes Post Office mail for a limited time — and leaves everything else exactly where it was.

This article is the checklist you were never given: the places your address actually lives, what happens when you miss one, and how to make the whole problem disappear the next time you move.

## What the Post Office Actually Does

When you file a change of address, USPS forwards eligible mail from your old address to your new one. It is free to file at a post office, or $1.25 online for identity verification. Forwarding can begin within three business days, though USPS says to allow up to two weeks. And it arrives piece by piece — which is why your mail shows up in bursts for weeks.

It also has hard limits:

- **First-Class mail and periodicals** (letters, bills, magazines) are forwarded free.
- **Priority Mail and USPS Ground Advantage** packages are forwarded free.
- **Media Mail** is forwarded, but you pay the shipping cost.
- **Marketing Mail** — the catalogs, flyers, and credit-card offers that fill most mailboxes — is **not forwarded at all**. It is thrown away.
- **Standard forwarding lasts 12 months.** You can pay to extend it in 6-, 12-, or 18-month blocks, with 18 months as the absolute maximum. After that, USPS returns your mail to the sender for six months with a label showing your new address.
- **It only covers USPS.** UPS, FedEx, and DHL do not see your change of address. They keep delivering to the street address on the label — your old house — until you update them yourself.

And here is the line USPS prints on its own page: your change of address order "only changes your mailing address with the Post Office. You must still update government agencies (like for your benefits, driver's license, and voter registration) and companies (like banks, insurance, online stores, etc.)."

In other words: the post office handles the mail it carries. Everything else is on you.

## The 20 Places Your Address Lives

Your address is not one record. It is dozens of them, scattered across banks, government agencies, employers, and subscription databases. When you move, each one has to be told — and most of them will not be told by the post office.

Walk through this list before your move, not after:

1. **Banks and credit unions** — checking, savings, and the address they mail statements to.
2. **Credit card companies** — every card, even the one you never use.
3. **Your employer and payroll** — W-2s and pay stubs go to the address on file.
4. **The IRS** — file Form 8822 (or Form 8822-B for a business). Processing can take four to six weeks, and the IRS warns that not all post offices forward government checks.
5. **The Ohio BMV** — drivers and vehicle owners must notify the BMV of an address change **within 10 days** in Ohio. A missed renewal notice can quietly cost you your registration.
6. **Voter registration** — Ohio requires you to register or update your address no later than **30 days before an election**. Do it at the Secretary of State's online portal before you forget.
7. **Social Security** — if you receive benefits, update your address with the SSA directly.
8. **Insurance** — auto, home, renters, health, and life. Your premium and your coverage can both be affected by where you live.
9. **Medicare, Medicaid, or VA benefits** — each is a separate record that does not move with you.
10. **Healthcare providers** — your doctor, dentist, pharmacy, and any clinic that mails you results or appointment reminders.
11. **Utilities** — electric, gas, water, internet, and the security system you may have forgotten about.
12. **Streaming and subscription services** — the ones that mail you anything: meal kits, magazines, razors, pet supplies.
13. **Online retailers** — Amazon, Walmart, and every saved address in every checkout you have ever used.
14. **Shipping carriers** — UPS, FedEx, and DHL accounts, plus their delivery preferences. This is the one that keeps biting people.
15. **Loans and mortgages** — the bank that holds your car loan or home loan sends statements monthly.
16. **Retirement and brokerage accounts** — 401(k)s, IRAs, and investment accounts mail tax forms every January.
17. **Professional licenses and certifications** — if you are licensed in Ohio for anything, the board needs your current address.
18. **Schools** — your kids' school, or your own, for emergency contacts and report cards.
19. **Alumni associations and memberships** — organizations that mail renewal notices and tax-deduction receipts.
20. **Warranties and registrations** — products you registered, receipts you asked to be mailed, rebates still in the pipeline.

That is twenty places minimum. Most people, moving for the first time, miss five or six of them. And the ones they miss are never the junk mail — they are the bills, the renewals, and the tax documents.

## What Missing One Actually Costs

Let's make this concrete. Miss the BMV renewal notice, and your tags go stale — a fix that involves fees and a trip to the deputy registrar. Miss the IRS change, and a refund check or a notice goes to a house you no longer own. Miss your bank, and statements containing account numbers sit in a mailbox in front of your old front door, visible to whoever lives there now.

Miss the carrier accounts, and the expensive part: a $900 package from UPS or FedEx delivered to your old address, left on a porch that is no longer yours. The carrier's own policy is that they delivered to the address on the label. Your change of address with the post office does not matter to them.

Then there are the quiet costs. The magazine that stops arriving because Marketing Mail is not forwarded. The subscription you forgot to update that keeps billing a card and mailing to the void. The voter registration you meant to update and now can't, because you found out the week before Election Day and Ohio's deadline was 30 days out.

None of this is the post office being cruel. It is simply a system designed for a world where people moved once and stayed put. When your life does not match that pattern, the system leaks — and the leaks always seem to happen at tax time.
