## What "Not a PO Box" Actually Means

When people type "mailbox not po box" into Google, they usually mean one of two things:

1. **They need a real street address.** A bank, an employer, the IRS, an LLC filing, a marketplace seller account — someone is telling them "we do not accept a PO Box number." They need an address that looks like a building, not a compartment.
2. **They need packages.** They are tired of driving to the post office to pick up oversized boxes at the counter, or tired of watching a FedEx or UPS driver skip their PO Box entirely because those carriers do not deliver to box numbers.

Both problems have the same fix: a private mailbox — sometimes called a PMB, a commercial mailbox, or a mailbox rental with a physical address.

## Why a PO Box Cant Do This

The PO Box is a good product with a hard ceiling, and the ceiling is the box number itself.

**USPS only.** FedEx, UPS, and DHL do not deliver to a PO Box number. Their systems route to street addresses. When a private carrier sees "PO Box 47" on a label, the package goes back to the terminal — it never even loads onto a truck. USPS does offer a Street Addressing option on some PO Boxes, but it is "if available" at each location, and private carriers only deliver there "as long as they comply with USPS mailing standards." It is an exception, not a guarantee.

**Size limits.** A PO Box holds letters and small parcels. When a package does not fit, USPS leaves a slip and you collect it at the counter during business hours — or from a parcel locker, where one exists.

**Signature is a detour.** Packages that require a signature get a notice in your box, and you go to the counter to sign.

None of that is a knock on the postal service. It is simply what a locked compartment in a post office wall is built for. If your mail is letters and small USPS parcels, a PO Box may be plenty. The moment you need a street address — or a FedEx box, a UPS box, a DHL box — you have outgrown it.
