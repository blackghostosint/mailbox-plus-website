## The Address You Keep Handing Out

The mechanism working against you is simple: your home address is the only address your paperwork has ever been given, so every system that touches your business copies it forward. It is not one leak. It is a chain of them, and each link looks reasonable on its own.

It starts with the state. When you file Articles of Organization with the Ohio Secretary of State, you list a principal office address and a statutory agent with an Ohio street address. That information goes into the state's business database, and the address is public record — the Secretary of State's own business search displays the statutory agent's name and address, and it tells you plainly that it does not keep owner lists but does list agent names and addresses. If you appointed yourself as your own statutory agent, you put your home street on that record yourself.

Then the chain spreads. Suppliers run commercial credit checks against your address. Your customers see it on invoices and quotes. If you sell online, the return address printed on every shipping label is the address in your account settings — which is your house. A data broker scraping public business records for a lead list does not care that the address is also where your family eats dinner. The record never asks.

None of this required you to be careless. The form asked, you answered honestly, and the systems downstream did what systems do: copied the answer forward.
