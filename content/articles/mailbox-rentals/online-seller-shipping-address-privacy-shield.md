## The Address Machine

This isn't one leak. It's a system, and every part of it is legal and routine.

Start with the Postal Service itself. Under the Domestic Mail Manual, a domestic return address must appear legibly on Priority Mail, USPS Ground Advantage, Package Services, insured mail, Certified Mail, and anything carrying an ancillary service endorsement — in practice, most of what an online seller ships. The rule exists so undeliverable pieces can come back to you. The side effect is that the sender's address is printed on the outside of nearly every box you send, readable by anyone who handles it or receives it.

Then the platforms build on top of that. On Etsy, your shipping profile's ship-from information becomes the return address that gets printed on your labels. On eBay, sellers keep return addresses on file in their Returns preferences — the help pages list a "registration address" and a "primary return address" among the options — and eBay's return shipping flow tells you to confirm the return address printed on the label. A buyer opening a return is one step away from seeing exactly where to send the box. That's the point of the feature. It just also means your address is doing the pointing.

Add the paperwork layer. In Ohio, every LLC must appoint a statutory agent, and the appointment — the agent's name and street address — is filed with the Secretary of State, which keeps a public record of it. The state's own business search FAQ is blunt about it: they don't list owners or officers, but they do list the statutory agent name and address, searchable by anyone. If you filed your own LLC and served as your own agent, your home address is in a public database right now.

And payment processors close the loop. PayPal's help pages state plainly that they can only confirm physical addresses, not PO Box addresses, for business verification. Amazon's Seller Central address verification asks for proof-of-address documents — bank statements, utility bills, lease agreements — tied to the business address on the account.

None of this is a scam. It's the machinery working as designed. The design just assumes the sender has an address they don't mind publishing. Most home-based sellers don't.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

You never argued that sellers shouldn't have a return address. You know carriers need one so undeliverable boxes come back, platforms need one so returns have somewhere to go, and the state needs an agent address so a lawsuit has someone to serve.

But needing an address is not the same as needing _your_ address. The system requires that an accountable, reachable, serviceable address exists at the end of the chain. It never said that address had to be the building where your kids do their homework. The two jobs — a public, accountable business address and a private family home — got welded together only because, for a long time, the PO Box was the only cheap alternative, and a PO Box can't do the public-facing half of the job.

## What You Lose by Staying on Your Home Address

**Time and worry, spent re-checking.** Every workaround you've invented — taping over labels, shipping from a relative's house, stripping the return address and hoping it sails through — costs attention. Return address requirements don't bend because you're uncomfortable; USPS Ground Advantage and Priority Mail require one, and an eBay return that can't reach you turns into a case you'll lose.

**The PO Box option, which fails at the carrier wall.** A PO Box does keep your home off labels for USPS-only shipping. But by Postal Service rule, only mail and official USPS notices go into a PO box — UPS and FedEx cannot deliver to one. UPS's own shipping guidelines say it outright: UPS cannot deliver to a PO Box. USPS offers a "street addressing" enhancement that lets private-carrier packages come to the post office address, but it's subject to availability and restrictions, and it still ends in "PO Box" on the label — which verification systems like PayPal's explicitly won't accept as a physical address. Say the honest thing: a PO Box protects letters. It fails at packages, carriers, and verification.

**Privacy that compounds in one direction.** A home address on one label is forgettable. A home address in the Ohio Secretary of State's searchable records, on every label for three years, inside your eBay account settings and your payment processor's file — that's a permanent, cross-referenced footprint. Public-records removal services can scrub some of it later, slowly and for a fee. Not printing it in the first place is faster and free.
