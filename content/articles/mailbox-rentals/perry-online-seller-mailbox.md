## The Label Problem

Call the villain what it is: **The Home-Address Label**. Every major selling platform generates shipping and return labels from the address you have on file. For a home-based seller, that address is your house.

There's no conspiracy here — it's just how the default works. Set up a seller account, enter your home address, and the system stamps it on every label you print. Thousands of labels, all pointing back to your kitchen table. Marketplace help centers even instruct sellers to print return labels with their own address on them — that's the design, not a bug you can toggle off.

And it isn't just labels. Your address rides along on packing slips, invoices, and carrier manifests. Anyone who buys from you — or receives a misdelivered box — now knows where you sleep. The FTC lists stolen personal information as the raw material of identity theft, and a home address is one of the easiest pieces to harvest.

The mechanism is legal, ordinary, and almost never questioned. But once you see it, you can't unsee it.

## What It Actually Feels Like

You feel it in small moments. You're taping a box for a buyer three states away, and you catch your own street name on the label and think: _she now knows exactly where I live._

You feel it when a buyer asks for a return. You print the label, and the return route leads them down your road, past your mailbox, to your driveway. Most buyers are fine. You only need one who isn't — one angry customer, one dispute, one stranger standing at your door asking about a refund.

You feel it at delivery time, too. You're not home when the truck comes, so the package sits on the porch in the rain, or worse, it disappears. Ask around Lake County and you'll hear the porch-pirate stories. Every one of those packages was addressed to a house nobody was watching.

And underneath all of it, a quieter cost: your business looks like what it is — a side hustle run from a kitchen. There's no separation between the person and the business, and buyers notice, even if they never say so.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

Your home address is not a business asset — it's a liability with a roof over it. You already expect privacy in the rest of your life. You don't print your phone number on your yard sign. You don't hand your schedule to strangers. Yet the default selling setup hands your home coordinates to every buyer, every carrier, every platform.

You also expect your mail to be _safe_. A package delivered to an empty house is not a delivery — it's a gamble. Signing for it, holding it, handing it to you across a counter — that's what a real delivery looks like.

And you expect to control where the business ends and your life begins. That line doesn't exist by default in e-commerce. You have to build it. It takes one decision: stop using your home address as your business address.
