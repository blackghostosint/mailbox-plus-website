## Why the Porch Is a Sitting Target

Here is what most people miss about porch pirates: they are not bold criminals. They are opportunists, and your porch is built to their advantage.

The delivery system is designed for the carrier's convenience, not your security. The driver arrives, drops the package, snaps a photo as proof of delivery, and moves on to the next stop. Whether that box is still there forty minutes later is nobody's problem. The tracking says "Delivered." The carrier's obligation is done.

Meanwhile the package is sitting in plain sight, on a porch that anyone can walk up to in broad daylight. In Ohio, the math is on the thief's side. Under Ohio Revised Code Section 2913.02, theft of property valued under $1,000 is a first-degree misdemeanor — and most porch packages are worth less than that. Cross $1,000 and it becomes a fifth-degree felony, but the odds of being caught for a quick porch grab in the first place are low. The pirates know this. They treat your front step as a low-risk, high-reward pickup point.

Mail theft is a different story: stealing mail from the U.S. Postal Service is a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. Section 1708, punishable by up to five years in prison. That is why postal inspectors take mail theft seriously. But the thief does not need to touch your mailbox to take your packages. They just need your doorstep.
