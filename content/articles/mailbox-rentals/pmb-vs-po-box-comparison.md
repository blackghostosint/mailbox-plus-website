## Not Your Fault: The PO Box Barrier

Here is the mechanism that trips people up, and it is not a trick — it is how the system was built. A PO Box is a numbered slot inside a post office, and only the Postal Service delivers to it. Ask UPS why your box never got there and their address guidelines give you the answer in four words: they cannot deliver to a PO Box — a shipment addressed to one gets delayed, loses its service guarantee, and draws an address correction surcharge. FedEx prints the same warning: no deliveries to PO Box addresses, with a handful of rural exceptions.

Some post offices offer a workaround called Street Addressing — you address the package to the post office's street address with your box number, and USPS accepts it. But USPS says it is available at "some" locations, it is opt-in, and it still ends at the same counter on post office hours. So the PO Box is a fine letter-slot wrapped inside a delivery barrier: the cheaper the box looks online, the more expensive the workaround becomes when your first UPS return needs somewhere to land.

Add the rental terms. USPS PO Box service runs in three-month, six-month, or twelve-month prepaid increments, billed up front with a refundable key deposit. Cancel early and the prepaid time is gone. The clerk slides the laminated rate sheet across the counter. The fine print behind her lists the three carriers that won't deliver to a box number. The system was built when a package meant a paper-wrapped parcel handed to a postal clerk. The world changed. The box didn't. And you are the one paying the price for it.

Where the PO Box wins: if your mail is truly letters-only — bills, cards, statements — and you already pass the post office regularly, a small box is a perfectly good, inexpensive slot. If that's you, take the small box and don't apologize for it.

Where it loses: the first time a courier box needs somewhere to land, the first time a form demands a street address, the first time you prepay twelve months and then move.

## What Keeps Slipping Away

Stay with the wrong box and the costs compound quietly:

- **Time.** Every mis-delivered courier package becomes a second trip — to a depot, on depot hours, with a slip in your hand. An hour of your evening per incident, repeating.
- **Money.** A prepaid twelve-month box you abandon when you move is cash you handed over for months you will never use — up to $236 for the year on a size-2 box — plus the address-correction surcharges when a shipper tries your box number anyway.
- **Privacy.** A PO Box number does not work on forms that require a street address, so the boxes, filings, and registrations fall back to your home address — printed on every label, visible to every porch pirate and data broker.
- **Opportunity.** The online seller who lists a street address looks established. The one listing a box number looks temporary — and some platforms reject it outright.

None of this arrives as a crisis. It arrives as a slow drip of slips, rejections, and redo trips — the tax on a decision made before you knew the fine print.
