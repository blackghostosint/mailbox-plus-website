# Your Hambden Business Card Looks DIY. Your Customers Notice.

![A bent, inkjet-printed business card with jagged cut edges sitting on a table](articles/printing/hambden-graphic-design-featured.webp)

You run a landscaping company out of Hambden. You mow lawns, trim hedges, haul brush. You do good work. Your customers refer you.

The card you hand them is printed on a home inkjet. The corners are rounded from your wife's scrapbooking scissors. The logo is a clip-art tree next to your phone number. The paper is standard office stock — 24-pound, bright white, bends when you hold it by one corner.

The card says something about you. It says you do not take your own marketing seriously. If you do not take your card seriously, why would a customer trust you with their yard?

## What a DIY Card Tells the Customer

A customer pulls out your card at the kitchen table. They look at it. They look at the card from the roofer who handed them a thick stock, full-color, rounded-corner piece with a clean logo and a website that works on a phone.

They put both cards in the junk drawer. But one stays in the wallet.

The card they keep tells them the roofer runs a professional operation. The card they toss tells them you ran low on inkjet paper and printed a batch at 11 p.m.

You did not mean to send that message. But the card sent it anyway.
