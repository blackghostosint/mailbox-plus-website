## The Trip You Keep Dreading

You know the card we're talking about. The one you printed at home in 2019 on cardstock that curls at the corners, the one where the phone number sits a little too close to the edge. You hand it out anyway, and every time you do, there's that half-second where you almost apologize before the person even looks at it.

It shouldn't work like that. You sent the invoice for the Hartley job, you did the work, you got paid. The card in your wallet should be able to prove all three in a glance.

## Not Your Fault: The DIY Design Gap

The export window is already set to screen resolution, RGB color, no bleed. Nobody checks it. The online shop prints that file anyway, takes your money plus shipping, and mails back 500 cards with a 0.125-inch crop where your phone number used to be. Legal — and not your mistake. That's the No-Bleed Default: a system that sells you a print of your own unfinished file and calls it done. They need a file that looks finished on a phone. You need 250 cards and a banner before Saturday.

## The Sigh You Make at the Counter

Maybe you've already tried. You sat down with the app one evening, dragged a logo around for an hour, and got something _close_ — but the text was crowded and the flyer never lined up. You closed the app and told yourself you'd finish it this weekend.

You're at a job site, you reach for your wallet, and the card sticks to the cheap laminate — you peel it free, thumb pressed on the curled corner, and hear yourself say "I've got a card" in a voice that's already apologizing. A customer asks if you have a flyer they can pin up at their office and you say, "I can email you something," and then never do. Every week the DIY version stays in circulation, it's out there meeting new customers for you.

## This Wasn't Supposed to Be This Hard

Think about how small the actual task is: you already know your business, your customers, and your name. The information takes two minutes to say out loud.

But the current system asks you to do all four jobs yourself, and you can see it at 11:40 at night: the laptop glow, the export box still saying Final_v3, the bleed checkbox you've never been sure about, the banner size still a guess. A two-minute conversation gets stretched into three evenings of that, and the end result still looks homemade. That's backwards. The part that's genuinely hard should be done by someone who does it every day; the part that's genuinely easy — telling someone about your business — should stay yours. Nobody signed up to learn prepress. You signed up to run a business.

## What We Hear From Mentor Every Week

Most weeks, somebody from Mentor is at our counter telling a version of that story, and they all end the same way — with relief that they never had to learn the software part. Here's what makes that possible. We're independent, so there's no franchise script between you and the person doing the work. The designers and the presses share the same building, so the file that gets laid out is the file that prints — no handoff between strangers, no warehouse two states away. And because we also handle shipping and mail for businesses all over Lake County every day, we know what a card actually gets used for: it travels. It gets shoved in trucks, stuck on corkboards, mailed in envelopes. We design for that life, not for a portfolio shot.

## The Whole Process, Nothing Hidden

Here's the whole conversation, so there are no surprises. She'll ask three things: who the customer is, what the piece has to do, and which of the old cards you liked best — even the DIY one had something right about it. Then she takes the logo off your phone and rebuilds it as a clean vector file, the kind that stays sharp at any size, so the same mark works on your card, your truck door, and the banner — or on the [EDDM mailers](/articles/chardon-eddm/) that put your flyer on every doorknob in a neighborhood. You approve a [printed proof](/copy-print/graphic-design/) at the counter, not a thumbnail on a screen — you hold it, you say what's off, and that corrected proof is the version that runs. Copies and everyday printing run on our own machines here; business cards, posters, and custom prints are handled through our print partner, so you're never the one chasing a vendor. You leave with a pickup time instead of a login, and when you come back, the finished pieces are sitting at the counter waiting.

## What Keeps Slipping Away

There's a corkboard on Center Street with a competitor's clean card pinned where yours should be. Yours is still in the shoebox, curling — and when the new pieces land, the old ones can go straight into [shredding](/articles/mentor-shredding/). The cardstock you threw out still cost you, and the Saturday you gave the app was a Saturday you didn't bill.

None of this is a character flaw. The design work just had nowhere to go but your own evenings — the only hours your business never gets back.

## The Version Where It Works

The shoebox stays on the counter. She slides the curled card aside, looks at your logo on your phone. "You don't need to bring me a file. That's my half."

Ten minutes later you leave with a proof time instead of a homework list. When you come back for pickup, the stack is wrapped and sitting at the counter, the ink dry, the corners trimmed square. And the next week, you pull the card out. It doesn't peel off the one behind it. The corner is square. He reads the number out loud and puts it in his shirt pocket. You don't narrate the card. There's nothing to apologize for, because there's nothing unfinished about it — the file that went to print was checked by a person who does this every day.
