# Your Hambden Copier Jammed. Hand Us the Originals.

![A self-serve copier screen blinking an error code in fluorescent office light](articles/printing/hambden-copies-featured.webp)

The copier in the Hambden library blinks error code C-3451. Paper jam in tray 2. You open the side panel. You pull at the crumpled sheet. A strip of toner-blackened paper tears off in your hand. The rest stays wedged somewhere behind the drum.

You have thirty pages to copy and a meeting in forty minutes.

This is the self-serve tax — you do the work, you fix the jam, you buy the paper, you refill the toner, you stand there watching the green bar crawl across the screen. The price per page is lower. The price in time is higher.

## What You Do at a Self-Serve Machine

You feed pages one at a time. You sort the output yourself. You reload the paper tray when it runs out. You stand there.

The machine counts every page. The machine charges your card. The machine breaks. You fix it.

You never planned to become a copy-machine technician. But that is the deal when you walk up to a self-serve kiosk. You get the machine. You get the problems. You get the blinking error code.

## What We Do Instead

At **Mailbox Plus**, there is no self-serve counter. There is a counter with a person behind it. You walk in with your originals. You hand them over. You tell us how many copies you need. Single-sided or double-sided. Color or black-and-white. Stapled or loose.

We make the copies. We check the quality. We hand them back.

No error codes. No paper jams. No standing in front of a machine watching a progress bar.

You walk in, you hand us the stack, you walk out with the copies. It takes less time than clearing one jam on the library machine.

## The Numbers

A self-serve copy at the library is ten cents a page. Black-and-white. You do the work.

At Mailbox Plus, [black-and-white copies](/copy-print/copies/) start at a comparable rate. But you do not stand there. You do not fix the jam. You do not sort the pages. You hand us the stack. You get back to your day.

You need thirty copies of a five-page bid for a Hambden Township property. That is 150 pages. On a self-serve machine, you stand there for fifteen minutes feeding pages. At Mailbox Plus, you hand us the originals and come back to a finished stack.
