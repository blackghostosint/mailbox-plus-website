## The File-to-Paper Gap

Call the villain what it is: the File-to-Paper Gap.

Your screen produces color with light. Paper produces it with ink. Those are physically different systems, and the translation between them — the conversion from the RGB color your monitor shows to the CMYK ink a press lays down — is where bright blues go dull and reds go muddy. Professional designers build in that conversion. Everyone else discovers it after the print run, when the money is already spent.

The gap has three more traps behind the first one:

**Resolution.** An image pulled from a website can look flawless on a phone and be one-quarter the resolution a business card needs. On screen, the software smooths it over. In ink, every missing pixel shows as fuzz.

**Bleed and margins.** Printers cannot hit an invisible edge exactly, so a design that runs to the card's border needs extra artwork beyond the trim line. A file built without bleed either prints with a sliver of white on the edge or gets trimmed so close the text lives dangerously near the blade.

**The upload-and-pray workflow.** The big online print mills price by volume and automate preflight, which means an algorithm checks your file — not a person who knows what you are printing, what it is for, and calls you before the run when something looks wrong. The proof you approved on their website is rendered by the same automated pipeline that will print the error. The convenience is real. So is the silence when something is wrong.

None of this is bad luck. It is a system built to move files, not to catch mistakes.
