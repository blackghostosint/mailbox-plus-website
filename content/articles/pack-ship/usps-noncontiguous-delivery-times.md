## Your Daughter's Box Is Due Friday. USPS Just Moved the Goalposts.

Your daughter's box for Anchorage is on the counter. It is due Friday — her birthday is the following week, and you promised. The clerk's screen says what it has said for years: a few days, five at most. You pay and you leave.

Except the Postal Service changed the fine print on October 1, and the number for a box like yours is no longer a few days. It is "10 days or more." Some lanes even longer. The notice that carried it was a PostalPro bulletin most shippers never open.

## Here's the Number That Changed Under You

You paid under the old promise. USPS had already filed the new one on September 25. It covers packages moving to and from Alaska, Hawaii, and the U.S. territories. Two services you probably use just got slower:

**USPS Ground Advantage** — you know the 2-5 day promise it's been sold on? For Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, and the island territories, USPS just replaced it with a **10-days-plus service expectation** — and says some lanes "may be significantly longer than 10 days."

**Priority Mail** — long quoted as 2-3 days — is now **2-4 days** to or from Alaska, Hawaii, American Samoa, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, and **2-6 days** to or from the Pacific territories.

And here's the buried kicker: if your box is under a pound, USPS says it will "primarily" move by surface transportation — the same slower lane heavier packages already used. You paid before the new standard was published — you did not misread a label.

Whether Priority's days saved are worth the upcharge depends on your lane and your deadline — compare the two quoted dates before you pay.
