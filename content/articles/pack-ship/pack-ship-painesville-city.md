## The Box on Your Passenger Seat Has Been There Since Tuesday

You know this box. It is the one you packed three nights ago, carefully, on the kitchen table — your mother's china for your sister in Carolina, or the eBay sale that the buyer is already messaging you about. It is taped on one seam. The other seam you left open because you ran out of tape, and because you are not entirely sure the box is right anyway.

And so it rides. To work and back. Past the post office you do not have forty minutes for. Past the pharmacy where they turned you away last time because the box was not fully sealed and their clerk is not allowed to touch it.

Here is the part nobody tells you: the box is not the problem. The problem is that Painesville's shipping options quietly force you to choose between a counter that can only sell you one carrier's opinion and a plastic bin that nobody is watching. Neither one of those is a shipping solution. One is a sales booth. The other is a bet.

## The Two Doors Painesville Offers You — and Why Both Are Bad

Door number one is the single-carrier franchise. Walk in and you get one network, one rate sheet, and one opinion. The clerk cannot tell you that a rival carrier delivers to your exact zip code sooner for less — not won't, _can't_. Their job is their own label, on their own box, at their own price.

Door number two is the unstaffed drop box or the pharmacy counter. This one is worse, because it looks free. Nobody tells you that handing a parcel to an unattended bin means zero proof of custody until a driver scans it, hours later, if it fits. And if it does not fit — if a seam is open, if the box is a couple inches over some limit you never got told about — the answer comes from a cashier whose actual job is prescriptions, not parcels.

So the errand that should take five minutes becomes a drive across town, a line, a maybe. And the box goes back on the passenger seat.
