## The Franchise Monopoly and the Drop-Box Trap

Call the mechanism what it is: the Single-Carrier Funnel and the Unstaffed Drop-Box Trap. It is not an accident that shipping a box out of downtown Painesville feels so complicated. It is the natural consequence of how modern carrier retail networks are structured.

When you walk into a dedicated corporate franchise or carrier store, you are dealing with a closed ecosystem. A single-carrier counter will never tell you that the rival carrier across town is delivering to that exact Chicago or Pittsburgh zip code for twelve dollars less, or that another service will get it there a full day earlier without charging an exorbitant express priority fee. They are contractually obligated to sell you their own network rate, regardless of whether it fits your deadline or budget.

The alternative—unstaffed drop boxes and pharmacy pickup counters—is even worse. Over the past decade, national carriers have aggressively offloaded retail customer service onto convenience stores, pharmacies, and grocery counters. But a convenience cashier’s primary job is ringing up prescriptions and retail merchandise. They do not have packaging supplies, they do not cut custom cartons, and they cannot verify dimensions. If your parcel is two inches over the standard limit, or if you need a strip of heavy-duty reinforced tape to seal an open seam, you are turned away. Even worse, leaving a high-value parcel in an unattended bin leaves you with zero proof of physical custody until a driver eventually scans it hours later.

You are caught between two bad choices: pay inflated rates at a single-carrier franchise or gamble on an unsupervised drop point that leaves you holding an unsealed box.
