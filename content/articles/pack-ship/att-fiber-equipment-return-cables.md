## The Part Nobody Warns You About

You canceled your AT&T Fiber service — the call was easy, the rep pleasant.
Then you looked down at the pile on your table —
the white gateway, a tangle of cables, two power cords — and one question
would not leave you alone: how much of this actually has to go back?

Some box up everything,
including cables AT&T never wanted, and drive somewhere they did not need to
go. Others leave the gateway in a drawer, tell themselves they will deal with
it later, and get a $150 non-return fee on their final bill for the privilege.
The confirmation email never spells out the line between "return" and "keep."

Here is the short version: the gateway and its power supply go back. Your
cables do not. You'll make two stops. First ours — we pull the keep
pile out. Then a FedEx Office or company-owned UPS Store counter, where
they scan it loose and hand you a receipt.

## The Cable Guessing Game: How It Got This Way

The confusion is not your fault. It is built into how AT&T hands you the
equipment in the first place.

When the technician installed your fiber, the bag of parts he left behind
mixed two categories of gear together, and nothing was labeled. There was
AT&T-owned equipment — the Wi-Fi gateway (a BGW320 or BGW210 for most Fiber
homes), maybe an AirTies Wi-Fi extender if you signed up for extended Wi-Fi
coverage — and there was the loose hardware: ethernet cables, an HDMI cable,
power strips, the odds and ends that live behind your TV. AT&T leases the
first category and effectively gives you the second. Nothing in the install
tells you that.

Then you cancel, and the return instructions arrive as a confirmation email
with a fee warning, a support page with a two-column table, and a 21-day
clock that started the moment you disconnected. If you read the table
carefully you will find the answer — AT&T's own list of what not to return
names "equipment you own, third-party (non-AT&T) extenders, HDMI cables, or
ethernet cables" — but most people never see the table. They see the fee
amount and start guessing. Guessing wrong either way costs you: a wasted
trip, or a fee for hardware still sitting in your drawer.

Call it the Disconnect-Date Clock: it starts the day you cancel — not
when the box arrives, not when you feel ready. It runs from the service
change or disconnect date, whether or not you knew it started. And the
fee is real: $150 for a Wi-Fi or All-Fi gateway, $200 for an All-Fi Hub,
$65
for a Wi-Fi extender that came with your extended Wi-Fi service. If the
equipment comes back damaged from rough handling, AT&T can add a
damaged-equipment fee on top. And the cruelest part: the fee schedule is
impossible to miss, while the one line that answers your actual question —
keep the cables — sits in a footnote of the return table.

## From Handoff to Receipt

Three steps, no homework:

1. **Gather what you have.** Leave it loose — do not box anything up, do
   not hunt for the original packaging, do not coil the cables into some
   tidy bundle. The gateway, the extender if you have one, and the power
   supply for each. That is it. Bring your account number (it is on your
   bill or the packing slip from the equipment box).
2. **Put it on the counter.** In two minutes we separate the AT&T gear
   from the keep pile and check your account number against the bill.
   The wall-mounted fiber jack and ONT never leave the wall — we'll tell
   you that before you touch them.
3. **Leave with the pile handled.** What's in your hand when you walk
   out is the scanned receipt — your proof against the fee, and the last
   time you have to think about this.
