## The Scene That Starts Everything

Saturday morning. You pour the coffee, open the laptop, and pull up your seller dashboard the way you do every morning — except this morning there is a banner you have never seen before. "USPS Ground Advantage rate update." You click it. Your stomach drops a little, because you know what "rate update" means when it appears without warning.

Rates went up. Yesterday. Effective immediately.

Not next quarter. Not after a sixty-day notice period. Yesterday, with less than twenty-four hours between the announcement and the new prices.

You have fourteen live listings with shipping folded into the price. A stack of labels priced at the old rates. And a quiet understanding that every package you ship from here on costs a little more than you budgeted.

It is ten cents on a three-to-five-pound Ground Advantage package, at first glance. Ten cents is nothing, right? One seller on the eBay boards put it better: the ten cents is not the problem. The problem is that USPS can now change the price of shipping with less than a day's notice — and there is not a thing you can do about it.

## The Silent Rate Hike

Here is what happened, in plain terms.

On July 12, 2026, USPS put through a set of rate changes it actually announced. Fine. That is how it is supposed to work. But two weeks later — on Friday, July 24 — eBay warned sellers that USPS was raising Ground Advantage rates again, effective the very next day. eBay Labels rates for Ground Advantage packages weighing three to five pounds went up ten cents across every shipping zone.

That increase came through a program you have probably never heard of: USPS Connect eCommerce. It is the program that lets marketplaces and postage providers offer discounted rates to sellers like you. And here is the part that should make you sit up: USPS does not publicly announce rate changes for USPS Connect eCommerce. Sellers get the news when the marketplace gets the news, which is to say, the day before it happens.

eBay said it is "working closely with USPS" to help sellers stay ahead of the updates. That is a nice sentence. It does not lower your shipping costs.

USPS has been making more frequent rate changes this year — eBay said as much in its own announcement. Sellers on the discussion boards are counting this as the second rate increase in a single month. The pattern is not the price. The pattern is the silence.

## What It Actually Feels Like

Put yourself back in that Saturday morning chair. You ship maybe fifteen packages a week — a mix of clothing, collectibles, and the occasional awkward box that fits nothing. Your shipping is priced into your listings. You did that on purpose, because buyers respond to "free shipping" and you built your store around it.

Now do the math. Fifteen packages a week, an average of fifteen cents more each — with the rural-zone increases some sellers are reporting, it can be far more than that. That is two dollars a week. Eight dollars a month. A hundred dollars a year — gone, silently, from a line item you set months ago.

And because the rate changes hit with no warning, you cannot plan around them. You cannot quietly adjust prices next Tuesday. You find out the way everyone finds out: on a Saturday, after the rates have already changed, staring at a banner.

That is the feeling. Not outrage at ten cents. The feeling is that you are running a business on top of a pricing system you cannot see, cannot predict, and cannot question.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

A price change is a normal part of commerce. Nobody expects shipping to cost the same forever. But a price change with less than twenty-four hours' notice is not a price change — it is an ambush.

You run a real business. You set prices, you plan inventory, you promise delivery dates. Every one of those decisions depends on knowing what shipping costs. When the cost of shipping changes without notice, every one of those decisions is made on bad information.

Here is the principle: people who are asked to pay more deserve to know before they are asked. The Postal Service exists to serve the public. It does not get to move the price tag while your back is turned.
