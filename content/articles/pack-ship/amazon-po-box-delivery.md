## Why the Box Fails at Checkout

Let's be precise about why the PO Box fails, because nobody at Amazon or USPS explains it plainly.

A USPS PO Box lives inside a post office. USPS delivers into it. That's it. Amazon's own last-mile drivers — the Flex folks in their own cars — deliver to street addresses only. When an Amazon parcel ends up at a post office addressed to a box, it's because USPS handled the final leg, not Amazon. This is confirmed on USPS's own guidance: private carriers cannot deliver to a PO Box.

So when you enter a PO Box on Amazon, you're betting the whole order on the one carrier that can legally touch that box. And sellers shipping through Amazon Buy Shipping have reported — repeatedly, in documented seller-support escalations — that the USPS label options get filtered out for PO Box addresses, leaving only options that can't deliver there. When sellers pushed back, Amazon's position was that this was working as designed. Whether or not that's the official line, the practical result for you is the same: the order stalls, and you're the one who pays for the stall.

## Counting the Cost of a USPS-Only Address

Run the tape on what this costs you, order by order.

First, the failed order. You catch the error at checkout, re-enter a home or work address, and wait. Or you don't catch it, and the package bounces back to the seller while you wait for a refund to crawl through.

Second, the detour trip. When USPS does take the final leg, your parcel goes to the post office, not your door. You drive there during their hours, stand in line, and carry the box home. Do that a few times a month and you've donated real hours to a workaround.

Third, the signing problem. A PO Box doesn't come with someone accepting a signature-required delivery on your behalf and holding it safely until you arrive on your schedule. You're fitting your errands around their window.

None of these show up on a receipt, which is exactly why they keep happening. They're a tax paid in trips and do-overs.

## Priced Cheap, Built Narrow

Here's the part that stings. USPS pricing for PO Boxes starts roughly at $7 a month and climbs past $90 depending on box size and fee group, per USPS Notice 123. Cheap, yes — and priced that way because it does one job: holds flat mail and USPS-only parcels.

The moment you need a delivery that cannot be routed through USPS — such as a UPS or FedEx delivery or a carrier signature service — that box stops working, and you're back to improvising. The pricing looks like a bargain until you count the workarounds it forces. The rule set was written for the carrier that owns the box, not for you, the customer who has to receive from all of them.

That's the structural fix: Amazon, UPS, FedEx, DHL, and USPS all deliver to a street address. One address, every carrier, no exceptions to memorize.

Cost three: the growth tax. The moment you need signature acceptance, carrier choice, or reliable receipt of anything not shipped by USPS, the cheap box becomes the expensive option — because you're paying for it plus the workarounds around it.
