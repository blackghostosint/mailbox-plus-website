## The Counterfeit Label Machine

The gray market for "discounted postage" has been around for years. It works like this: a reseller obtains postage through a contract that does not actually allow resale — or fabricates the contract entirely — then sells the labels at a markup that still looks like a steal to you. From the outside it looks like a harmless money-saving hack. From the inside it is counterfeit postage, and it has been running on the quiet trust of the entire parcel network.

The Postal Service has decided that era is over.

According to a report the USPS Office of Inspector General published on August 3, the agency is leaning hard into artificial intelligence to defend itself — and its customers. The report describes more than 35 active AI use cases inside the Postal Service. The most consequential one so far targets exactly what you bought at 11:40 last night.

## What It Actually Feels Like

Here is the part that should make you sit up straight.

Since January, AI-driven detection has helped USPS identify and close **1,250 accounts generating counterfeit shipping labels**. That is not a threat written into a policy manual somewhere. That is a number from the current reporting period — accounts already shut down, transactions already flagged, labels already voided.

You do not have to be the one who printed the fake label to feel the fallout. The counterfeit operation does not absorb the loss — it disappears, and the question of who pays moves downstream. If your label was generated from a compromised or counterfeit source, and that source gets flagged, the label can be voided or the shipment examined. Your package. Your delivery promise to your buyer. Your reputation on the platform.

And you never see the machine coming. There is no warning email that says "we caught you buying from a gray-market reseller." There is just a label that stops working, a package that gets held, or a fee that lands somewhere you did not expect.
