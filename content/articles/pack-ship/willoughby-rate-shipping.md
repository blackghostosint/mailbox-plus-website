## The One-Rate Trap

Here's the mechanism: most shipping stores you can name are franchises of a single carrier. A carrier-branded franchise can only sell that carrier. It isn't hiding the cheaper option out of spite — it structurally _can't_ show it to you. If UPS is 40% more than USPS Ground Advantage for your four-pound box to Texas, you will never hear that at a UPS store, because UPS rates are the only rates on the screen.

And because there's no comparison happening, there's no pressure to compete for your box. The rate you're quoted is simply the rate. Take it or drive elsewhere — which most people, reasonably, don't.

Multiply that by every box you ship. If you send even five packages a month and the true best rate would have saved you an average of a few dollars each, that's real money — $180 or more a year — quietly absorbed because the comparison never happened in front of you.

And it's not just the base rate. Carriers layer on surcharges that change from year to year — delivery area fees, fuel adjustments, residential surcharges — and a single-carrier counter passes those along without ever mentioning that a different carrier's surcharge structure might have treated your address differently. You're not just comparing one price against another price. You're comparing two entirely different stacks of fees, and you're only ever shown one stack.

## What It Actually Feels Like

You're at the counter with your box. The clerk weighs it, taps at the register, and names a number. You want to ask "is that the cheapest way?" but you already know the answer: they sell one carrier, so of course it's the cheapest _they_ have. You pay it. In the car you pull out your phone, open a carrier's rate calculator, and confirm what you suspected — the other guy would've been $6 cheaper. You can't prove it for every box. But you feel it, and the feeling is right.

Here's the thing: your package doesn't care which logo is on the label. A four-pound box going to Phoenix has a cheapest correct answer, and that answer depends on weight, dimensions, zone, and speed — not on which franchise happens to be in your strip mall. The counter showing you one price isn't serving you; it's serving its parent brand.

This bites hardest if you sell online. When you're shipping a couple of orders a week from your spare room, every dollar of shipping comes straight out of your margin — you already set your prices around what the label costs, and you can't raise them every time a rate creeps up. A buyer in California doesn't pay your surcharge; you do. That's exactly why eBay and Etsy sellers are the people who benefit most from seeing all four rates before the label prints.
