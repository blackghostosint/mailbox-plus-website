## The Single-Carrier Screen

Call the mechanism what it is: the Single-Carrier Screen. Not a scam — a business model, operating in plain sight.

A franchise shipping store signs a contract with one carrier. That contract decides what the clerk's screen can show you, and what it can show you is that carrier's retail rate. If another carrier would carry your box to the same address for noticeably less, that number does not exist inside the store. Nobody lied to you. Nobody had to. The cheaper rate simply never got printed.

And the screen's pricing is not the floor, either. Franchise stores buy their rates at retail and resell them to you at retail-plus — a markup on top of a rate that was already the highest version of itself. You are not just paying the wrong carrier's price. You are often paying a premium for the privilege.

The system works because it assumes you will not comparison-shop. And you won't — not because you're lazy, but because comparison-shopping means driving to three different counters in Mentor with the same box and asking three different clerks to quote the same shipment. Nobody does that. The system is built on that certainty.

## What It Actually Feels Like

It feels like a small doubt you swallow. The clerk turns the screen around, there's a number, you pay it. Driving home you think: was that good? You genuinely don't know. There was nothing on the screen to compare it to.

Multiply that by every box you've ever shipped. Birthday packages to your sister in Columbus. The eBay sale you packed in your kitchen. Returns, gifts, care packages. Every one of them closed with the same shrug — "I guess that's what it costs."

It isn't what it costs. It's what one store, tied to one carrier, decided to charge you.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

You should be able to see every carrier's rate for your package and pick the best one. That is not a radical demand — the carriers publish their rates, the packages are weighed on the same scale, and the comparison takes seconds on a computer that is already sitting behind the counter. The only thing missing is the will to show you.

Transparency shouldn't be a premium feature. The person shipping a five-pound box to Cleveland and the person shipping a thirty-pound box to Phoenix both deserve to see the whole board before paying a dime.
