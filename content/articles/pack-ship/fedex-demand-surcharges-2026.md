## The Verdict

Picture the label on your screen in the second week of December. Base rate. Standard Additional Handling fee. Then a line you have not seen since summer: **Demand — Additional Handling Surcharge: $11.85**. Then the fuel percentage, recalculated on all of it. Nothing about your box changed — the calendar did.

That is the FedEx demand surcharge: not a mystery fee, but a published, dated, three-window price increase running **Sept. 28, 2026 through Jan. 17, 2027**. In the most expensive window (Nov. 23 – Dec. 27), the Demand — Additional Handling Surcharge is **$11.85 per package** and the Demand — Oversize Charge is **$117.25 per package**, charged on top of the standard handling fees your box already pays and the weekly fuel surcharge on top of that.

You cannot negotiate it, opt out of it, or talk FedEx out of the calendar. What you can do is ship around it: move volume out of the Nov. 23 window, keep your packages out of the surcharge trigger zones, and compare all four carriers before you pay — because UPS and USPS run their own peak pricing, and they do not peak at the same prices. Here is the whole stack, with numbers, so you can see exactly where your money goes.

## The 2026-2027 Demand Surcharge Table

Straight from FedEx's published schedule (updated Sept. 4, 2026):

| Surcharge                          | Sept 28 – Nov 22 | Nov 23 – Dec 27 | Dec 28 – Jan 17 |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| Additional Handling                | $8.80            | **$11.85**      | $8.80           |
| Oversize                           | $95.75           | **$117.25**     | $95.75          |
| Unauthorized Charge (Ground)       | $535             | **$595**        | $535            |
| Express overnight services         | $1.30            | **$2.55**       | $1.30           |
| FedEx 2Day services                | $1.20            | **$2.35**       | $1.20           |
| Ground residential & Home Delivery | $0.50            | **$0.80**       | $0.50           |
| Ground Economy (contract)          | $2.55            | **$4.05**       | $2.55           |

Two things about that table that most summaries get wrong:

**The start dates are not the same.** The handling, oversize, and unauthorized charges began **Sept. 28**. The per-package fees on Express, 2Day, ground residential, and Ground Economy do not start until **Oct. 26**. If you compared a receipt from mid-October to one from December and only saw the $0.80 residential fee, you were not hallucinating — that fee simply had not switched on yet.

**The Unauthorized Charge got a new name.** Effective Sept. 21, 2026, FedEx renamed the Demand — Ground Unauthorized Package Charge to the Demand — Unauthorized Charge. Same idea: it is what FedEx assesses when a package moves through its ground network without proper authorization. At **$595** during peak, it is not a fee — it is a punishment. If a drop-off location cannot take your package, the answer is a counter that can, not a gamble on an unauthorized channel.

## Which Window You Are In — and Which One Hurts

The three windows are not equal, and the expensive one is aimed squarely at the weeks you cannot skip:

- **Sept. 28 – Nov. 22:** the setup. Handling and oversize fees are already live. If your holiday shipping can move into this window, you pay the low column.
- **Nov. 23 – Dec. 27:** the squeeze. Every fee in the table jumps to its peak number, and this is exactly Cyber Week through the last shipping days before Christmas. This is where the calendar tax lives.
- **Dec. 28 – Jan. 17:** the aftertaste. Rates snap back down, but they do not disappear — and this is when returns and the stragglers ship.

One week matters more than any other on this calendar: the line between **Nov. 22 and Nov. 23**. A box shipped the Saturday before Thanksgiving and the same box shipped the Monday after can differ by the full peak column — $3.05 on a handling-triggered package, $21.50 on oversize — for identical labels.

## How the Fees Stack (the Part That Actually Costs You)

The demand surcharge never rides alone. Here is the full stack on a problem box in December:

1. **The base rate** — already up 5.9% this year from the January GRI, and set for another 5.9% average increase on Jan. 4, 2027.
2. **The standard surcharge** — if your box already trips FedEx's ordinary Additional Handling fee (heavy, long, or awkward), you pay that fee first.
3. **The demand surcharge** — the second, calendar-triggered copy of that same fee.
4. **The weekly fuel surcharge** — a percentage applied on top, which means it taxes the surcharges too.

That is why the same box can cost dramatically more in December without a single thing about it changing. The demand fee is charged **on top of** the standard fee, not instead of it — you pay both.

An oversize package is one that exceeds **96 inches in length, or 130 inches in length plus girth** — length plus twice the height plus twice the width, always rounded up. One guitar, one lamp, one set of golf clubs, and you are in the oversize club, paying a fee that has climbed from $62.50 in 2021 to $108.50 in 2025 and now $117.25 — nearly doubled in five years.

## FedEx vs. UPS vs. USPS at Peak

None of the big three skips peak pricing anymore, and they do not peak at the same prices. Here is how the 2026-2027 seasons line up:

|                                | FedEx                            | UPS                   | USPS                                        |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Peak window                    | Sept. 28 – Jan. 17               | Through Jan. 16, 2027 | Oct. 4 – Jan. 17                            |
| Additional handling            | $8.80 → **$11.85**               | $8.75 → **$11.90**    | n/a (flat per-package increases)            |
| Oversize / large package       | $95.75 → **$117.25**             | $96.25 → **$117.50**  | n/a                                         |
| Ground residential per-package | $0.50 → **$0.80** (from Oct. 26) | $0.50 → **$0.75**     | $0.40 – $20.80 range, tiered by zone/weight |

USPS filed its third straight holiday price change on **Aug. 25, 2026** with the PRC — flat per-package increases from **40 cents to $20.80** depending on service and zone, effective Oct. 4 through Jan. 17. For small, dense boxes headed to nearby zones, USPS still usually wins the peak season. For the big, weird, heavy boxes, the spread between UPS's $117.50 and FedEx's $117.25 is a rounding error — the real decision for oversize is whether the package trips the fee at all. ## The New Fee Most Sellers Have Not Heard Of

Buried in FedEx's schedule is a second demand mechanism aimed at high-volume shippers: the **Demand — Residential Delivery Charge**. It is dynamic — recalculated weekly from a "peaking factor" — and it applies to enterprise shippers moving more than **20,000 residential and Ground Economy packages in a week**. Per-package, it ranges from **$1.70 to $8.00** on ground services and **$3.05 to $9.35** on express, depending on how far that week's volume spikes above the shipper's June baseline.

That dynamic fee is a preview of where carrier pricing is going — away from fixed published surcharges, toward weekly volume-triggered charges a small shipper cannot see coming. The defense is the same at any volume: know what your box costs _this week_, not what it cost in September.

## What Actually Lowers the Bill

You cannot use the calendar against FedEx — no negotiation, no opt-out — but three real levers control the receipt:

**1. Ship before Nov. 23.** The whole holiday list that can go early, should. Every box moved out of the peak column saves the difference on its fee row. If you run an online shop, it also means pricing your December shipping quotes in November's rates and saying so on the checkout page.

**2. Stay out of the trigger zones.** The handling and oversize fees are dimension and weight thresholds, which means they are packable-around. A box that comes in at 94 inches instead of 97 skips a $117.25 fee. The right carton, the right padding, and a tape gun are the cheapest surcharge insurance you can buy — and if you would rather not gamble your geometry, that is literally our job (see [professional packing](/pack-ship/professional-packing/)).

**3. Compare carriers before you pay.** The surcharge tables differ by carrier _and_ by week. The cheapest option for your box in the Nov. 23 window may not be the cheapest in January. ## FAQ

### When do FedEx demand surcharges end in 2027?

The 2026-2027 demand surcharge windows end Jan. 17, 2027, and per-package rates snap back to their off-peak levels that day ($8.80 handling, $95.75 oversize). Note that FedEx's 2027 general rate increase of 5.9% takes effect Jan. 4, 2027 — so base rates step up while peak fees step down.

### Does the FedEx demand surcharge apply to every package?

No. The Additional Handling, Oversize, and Unauthorized charges apply only to packages that meet those criteria — heavy, oversized, or moving without authorization. The per-package fees ($0.50-$0.80 residential, $1.20-$2.55 express tiers) apply to every eligible package in their window, excluding FedEx One Rate packages.

### Which is worse in 2026, UPS or FedEx peak surcharges?

Nearly identical at the top of the table: UPS's peak Additional Handling is $11.90 versus FedEx's $11.85, and large-package $117.50 versus $117.25. On ground residential per-package fees, UPS's $0.75 peak is slightly under FedEx's $0.80. USPS's tiered $0.40-$20.80 increases usually make it cheapest for small, dense, near-zone boxes.
