# That Family Heirloom From Hambden Needs to Cross the Country Safely

Your grandmother's china set has been in the cupboard on Taft Road since 1958. Fourteen pieces. Blue floral pattern. Your sister in Phoenix wants it now.

You find a box in the basement. You wrap a plate in newspaper. You tape the box shut and write the address in Sharpie. You drop it at the counter and hope.

That hope is the problem.

## What Happens Inside the Box

A package moves through a conveyor belt system. It drops three feet into a bin. It gets stacked under a 40-pound box of books. It rides in a truck that hits potholes on Route 44.

Newspaper does not stop any of that. Newspaper shifts. Newspaper compresses. Newspaper leaves your grandmother's china rattling against cardboard.

The carrier covers the loss at $100 if you do not declare value. Your grandmother's china is not worth $100. It is not worth $100 to replace. It is not replaceable.

## How Professional Packing Works

At **Mailbox Plus**, we do not use newspaper. We do not grab whatever box is lying around. We follow a process.

Every fragile item gets a double-walled box rated for the weight. We put two inches of bubble wrap on every side. We use foam peanuts or crumpled kraft paper to fill the void space — not air. Air moves. The item shifts.

We tape every seam with 2-inch acrylic tape. Not masking tape. Not duct tape. Acrylic tape holds through temperature changes. A package sitting in a hot truck or a frozen sorting facility keeps its seal.

The box gets labeled FRAGILE on all six sides. Not because the carrier reads it — because the paperwork chain starts with the label. If something breaks, the label is evidence you packed correctly.

## What That Looks Like at the Counter

You walk in with your grandmother's china in a canvas bag. We unpack each piece. We inspect for cracks you might have missed. We wrap each plate separately with two layers of bubble wrap — the small-bubble kind that holds tight, not the big-bubble kind that pops.

We build a cradle in the box. The plates sit upright, not flat. Flat stacking transfers weight through the stack. Upright distributes the impact to the frame of the box.

We fill every gap. We close and tape. We weigh it. We hand you a receipt with the declared value.

You leave knowing exactly what is in that box and exactly how it is protected.
