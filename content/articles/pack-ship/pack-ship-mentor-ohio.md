## You Know the Price Before the Clerk Types

If you ship from Mentor — Etsy orders, eBay boxes, client packages, birthday boxes to your son in North Carolina — you already know how the next counter visit goes. You hand over the box, the clerk types, and there is one number. You pay it. And somewhere in the back of your mind, the same small question: was that actually the cheapest way to send this?

You have never been shown the answer. The fix is not four websites and a Saturday afternoon of re-typing dimensions. It is one counter that weighs your box once and shows you UPS, FedEx, USPS, and DHL rates side by side — so you pick the best price instead of the only one you were offered. Here is how it works.

## The Single-Rate Screen

The thing standing between you and a better price is not a scam. It is a screen that only shows one carrier.

Walk into a single-carrier storefront — the franchise shipping stores, the branded print-and-ship counters — and you are standing inside a contract. The carrier sets the retail rates the store must charge, and the store sells that one carrier's services because that is all the agreement covers. Ask "is there anything cheaper?" and the honest answer is a shrug: their system cannot display a rival carrier's rate. The cheaper option is not hidden. It is structurally invisible — structurally deleted from the screen you are looking at.

The difference between what you pay and the best available rate for that exact box — often 20 to 40 percent on a given shipment, depending on the zone, the weight, and the service level — never appears on any receipt. It is just quietly gone.

Mentor has the counters. There is a single-carrier franchise store on Mentor Avenue, and a FedEx-owned print-and-ship center at 8390 Mentor Avenue. Both will ship your package competently. Neither will show you what the other carriers would charge for it — because neither can. So the choice Mentor shippers actually face is: accept one carrier's rate, or open four carrier websites at the kitchen table and become your own rate analyst. Most weeks, the box just gets shipped.

## What It Actually Feels Like

You watch the clerk type. The number comes up. Maybe it is fine. Maybe you wince and think about the weight, the box, the way the last one cost less.

You ask if there is a cheaper option. "That's the rate." You pay it, because the alternative — comparing four carrier sites, second-guessing dimensional-weight math you were never taught — costs more of your evening than the difference is worth. But the sting is real, and it repeats with every box. You are not being paranoid. You were shown one price by a counter contractually unable to show you the rest.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

You comparison-shop everything else. You drive past one gas station for another that is two cents cheaper. You keep three tabs open before you buy a $40 anything. Shipping is one of the biggest recurring costs of selling online — and it is the one purchase almost nobody comparison-shops, not because you are careless, but because no counter in Mentor has ever put the whole board in front of you.

You should be able to walk in with a box and watch every major carrier's real rate for that exact package appear on one screen. Then choose. Not choose which branded store to trust — choose the price. That is not a fantasy. It is just what happens at a counter that answers to its customers instead of to one carrier's franchise agreement.

## What You Lose by Staying With the Single-Rate Screen

**Money, on every box.** A markup you never see is still a markup you pay. Ship five or ten packages a week and it is not a rounding error — it is margin leaking out one label at a time, unrecoverable the moment it prints. Nobody sends you a bill for it. That is exactly the point.

**Options you were never shown.** The four carriers price different boxes differently — a service level that is overpriced for one package is the bargain for the next. A one-carrier counter makes that decision for you every single week. Over a year, you are not just paying more; you are shipping with one carrier's boxes and tiers instead of the right fit for each shipment.

**The repacking advice that lowers rates.** A counter loyal to one carrier sells you that carrier's boxes. An independent counter asks what you are shipping and puts it in whatever costs you less. That advice is worth more than any single shipment — and you cannot buy it at a counter that only sees one carrier.

Every week on the single-rate screen is a week of paying full price by default.
