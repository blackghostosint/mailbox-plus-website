## The Hundred-Dollar Default

The thing standing between you and a real recovery isn't carelessness — it's a line in the carriers' terms that almost nobody reads until they need it.

FedEx states it plainly: the first $100 of value in your shipment is included in your shipping rate at no extra charge as part of their standard $100 limit of liability. USPS builds up to $100 of insurance into Priority Mail and Priority Mail Express — but not every service carries that included coverage, so what you're protected for depends entirely on which label the clerk picks. UPS is the same story at the franchise counters: declare nothing, and your maximum reimbursement for the item's value is $100. Every one of these is a ceiling on what you're covered for, not a promise to make you whole. None of it is hidden — the pages are public. They're just written in language nobody reads while the line builds up behind them.

And here's the part that stings worse: declaring a value above the default isn't insurance the way most people hear the word. It's a claim against the carrier's liability ceiling, and it comes with rules — proof of value, packaging standards, exclusions. If the box wasn't packed to standard, the declared value you paid extra for can be worth nothing at claim time.

So the default covers almost nothing, and the upgrade covers almost nothing if the packing gives them an out. That's the system. It runs in plain daylight, every day, in every shipping counter in the county.

## What It Actually Feels Like

For most people, that sentence is where the recovery dies. You didn't know declaring was a separate step from shipping. You didn't know packing was part of the coverage. You thought the label was the promise. It never was.
