## The Name for What's Been Happening

The villain has a name: the landed-cost gap. Here's how it operates in broad daylight.

When a package crosses a border, the destination country charges duties and import taxes. Nobody argues the government is entitled to collect. The trap is in the TIMING of when the buyer finds out what they owe. If your checkout shows a product price and a shipping fee, but the duties and taxes get assessed later — after the buyer has already paid — then your buyer gets billed by the carrier for clearance. The carrier is the government's collection agent at the door, and the bill arrives with your store's name implied, because YOU sold them the item.

It gets worse for the seller. FedEx surveyed small and medium businesses last spring, and 68% of them said their customers get surprised by additional duties, taxes or fees at delivery at least some of the time. Read that again: better than two out of three sellers you'd ask are watching this happen to their buyers. On any given international order, it's a coin flip. And every one of those surprises costs you one of three things: a refund from your margin, a package refused at the border, or a buyer who never comes back and tells the review section why.

## This Wasn't Supposed to Be This Hard

A buyer should know the full price of an arrival before they click pay. Full stop. Domestic checkout shows you the all-in price before you pay. Cross-border delivery never got that memo — and the buyer blames the only human in the loop: you.

The technology to show a complete total has existed for years. The reason it hasn't been standard is that guaranteeing a landed cost means somebody has to absorb the risk of being wrong. Brokers calculated the fees but refused to stand behind the calculation. So the gap stayed open, and small sellers fell into it.

## The New Math at Checkout

Here is what just changed, in plain terms. FedEx announced a suite of international shipping tools called Global Trade Navigator, and the piece that matters to you is a Shopify app called FedEx Duty & Tax. Here is what it does:

- At checkout, your international buyer sees a **guaranteed** duty-and-tax total before they pay. The number they see is the number they owe. That is the promise, spelled out in the app's own listing: "The amount shown at checkout is your final price for duties and taxes."
- If the actual duties and taxes come in HIGHER than what was shown, FedEx covers the overage. You don't. Your buyer doesn't.
- If the actual charges come in LOWER, no refund is issued — the amount shown includes a service fee for that protection. That is the honest trade: you pay a small premium for the certainty, and the certainty is what keeps the sale alive.
- The app is $99 a month after a 14-day free trial, and it works with a checkout you may already have — if your store runs on Shopify, this bolts into the exact moment of truth: the pay button.

The larger suite around it is worth knowing even if you never install the app. FedEx's Trade Planner estimates duties and taxes before a label is ever created, and their Ship Manager now lets you review Harmonized System code classifications and customs values on each product. Translation: the industry is finally moving the customs math from the delivery door to the preparation desk — where mistakes are free and surprises are impossible.

And you have options. UPS launched a similar service, Global Checkout, that shows a guaranteed total before purchase. Zonos offers a Duty and Tax app for Shopify as well. The market made the fix competitive — which means you get to pick instead of settle. And if you're shipping even two or three international orders a week, one prevented refund or one saved chargeback pays for the whole month.
