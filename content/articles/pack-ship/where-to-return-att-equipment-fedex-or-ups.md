## You Have the Box on the Seat and a Deadline on the Bill

You canceled AT&T internet, the equipment is sitting there — gateway, power cord, maybe an extender — and the confirmation email gave you a clock: 21 days, or a fee of up to $200 lands on your final bill. You just need to know one thing: which counter actually takes this back and gets it credited.

The short version: AT&T runs its internet-equipment returns through a company-owned FedEx Office Pack & Ship or The UPS Store, where a rep scans the gear against your account number. That is the whole list. Here is how each option really works, which ones quietly fail, and what to do if you are holding a prepaid label instead.

## The Rules AT&T Actually Enforces

AT&T does not mail you a rules sheet with your cancellation. The real instructions live on a support page and in a PDF called "Equipment Return Instructions," and they split returns into two tracks.

**Track one — no box, no label.** Take your equipment in _unpacked_, along with your account number, to a company-owned FedEx Office Pack & Ship or a The UPS Store. A store rep scans it, packs it, ships it, and hands you a tracking receipt. That scan is the moment AT&T's system knows the equipment is coming back.

**Track two — the prepaid label.** AT&T's return instructions provide a prepaid UPS shipping label for the do-it-yourself track. You pack the gear yourself in the replacement box or any sturdy unmarked box, strip the old barcodes, tape it, stick the label on, and drop it at a UPS shipping location. AT&T's own PDF adds one blunt warning: UPS drop boxes are not acceptable — the box is too big for the slot. It has to go to a staffed location, and you keep the tracking number yourself.

Everything else — the post office, the blue mailbox outside the grocery store, the corner counter that just ships boxes — is outside AT&T's system. Nothing there talks to AT&T's return database.

## The Machine Behind The Scan-Signature Gap

Here is why the two-track system exists, and why the wrong counter burns people.

AT&T does not know you returned your equipment because a box arrived. It knows because a _transaction_ was recorded — your account number tied to your gear, scanned at a location AT&T's program recognizes. The UPS Store and FedEx Office are wired into that program. Their systems read the return, pack the device, and generate the receipt that proves you surrendered it inside the 21-day window.

A prepaid label, by contrast, is just postage. Drop the same box anywhere else that accepts UPS packages and the package may still physically reach AT&T's warehouse — but you are betting the timestamp on that journey against the fee clock. If the scan chain breaks, AT&T sees an account with equipment out and no clean return event, and the non-return charge appears on the bill. Now you are calling 800.288.2020 to argue a $150 charge with a tracking number as your only exhibit.

And the fee is not a bluff — it is priced: $150 for a Wi-Fi or All-Fi Gateway, $200 for an All-Fi Hub, $65 for a Wi-Fi extender or All-Fi booster that came with Extended Wi-Fi Coverage. That is per unit. An unplanned $200 line item is a worse outcome than a ten-minute drive to the right counter.

## You've Done This Math Before

The fee clock doesn't care that you canceled in good faith. It only cares whether a scan hit AT&T's system. You cancel service expecting the hard part to be over — and then the fine print turns the last step into a scavenger hunt. The instinct is to take the easy option, the counter down the street. The trap is that the easy option is the one that doesn't register the return.

## What the Clerks Behind the Counter Know

One question comes up constantly at any shipping counter in Northeast Ohio, ours included: _can I just return this AT&T box here?_ And the honest answer has two halves.

If you have AT&T's prepaid UPS return label and your equipment is already packed, yes — a staffed UPS shipping location can take that package, and a shipping counter that handles prepaid UPS drop-offs can take it too. Keep your tracking number and confirm the acceptance scan.

If you have _unpackaged_ equipment and no label, the scan-and-pack program is AT&T's two-partner network: company-owned FedEx Office Pack & Ship or The UPS Store. That is who AT&T names on its own support page, and their systems are the ones wired to credit your account. We will tell you which situation you are in before we take your box — that is the difference between a counter that wants your package and one that wants your return to actually clear.

One more thing the counter knows: what _not_ to return. Wall-mounted gear — the fiber jack, the ONT, anything a technician screwed in — stays with the house. Don't unmount it, and leave the green cable where it is. Each device goes back with its own power supply; per AT&T, anything you own stays with you. ## From Handoff to Receipt

Whichever track you're on, the pattern is the same — turn the return into a piece of paper in your hand:

1. **Start the return.** Call AT&T at 800.288.2020 with your account number so the return is flagged on your account before anything moves. (Not sure what has to go back? The full equipment checklist lives in [our AT&T return guide](/articles/att-equipment-return-guide/).)
2. **Pick your track.** Unpacked gear → FedEx Office or The UPS Store, account number in hand, let the rep scan and pack it. Prepaid label → pack it yourself in a sturdy unmarked box, no drop boxes, staffed counter only.
3. **Keep the receipt.** Tracking number or store receipt — the scan event is what stops the fee clock. Screenshot it, file it, don't trust the confirmation email to arrive.
