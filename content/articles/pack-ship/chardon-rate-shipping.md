## The Franchise Markup

The villain in this all-too-common Chardon shipping story isn't a person, but a pervasive, almost invisible system that quietly drains your wallet: **The Franchise Markup**. This isn't just about paying a few extra pennies; it's a systemic issue, a design flaw built into the very structure of many single-carrier franchise stores that dot our landscape, often the very places you naturally think to go for shipping.

Let’s be crystal clear about how this mechanism operates. These stores, while seemingly convenient, operate under a specific, restrictive business model. They are licensed to represent _one_ primary carrier – be it UPS, FedEx, or another. Their entire operational framework, their software, their branding, even their internal incentive structures, are built around that singular relationship. This creates what we call a "blackout" scenario, a deliberate omission of choice that leaves you, the customer, at a significant disadvantage.

When you walk into one of these single-carrier franchise locations, you’re not entering a truly competitive marketplace. You're entering a carefully controlled environment where your options are pre-selected and pre-priced. They don't _hide_ cheaper carrier options out of malice, perhaps, but their business model _prevents_ them from even showing them to you. How can they quote a FedEx rate if they're a UPS store? How can they offer USPS if they're exclusively FedEx? They can't. So, you are inherently locked into one carrier's pricing structure, without any comparison.

But it gets worse. Not only are you denied the benefit of competition between carriers, but these franchise stores also often apply their _own_ additional markup on the rates they do show. They have overhead, franchise fees, and profit margins to maintain, and a portion of that inevitably comes from adding a percentage onto the standard retail rates of their designated carrier. So, you're not just paying the carrier's rate; you're paying the carrier's rate _plus_ the franchise’s premium. This isn’t a personal failure on your part for not knowing; it’s a systemic limitation operating in plain sight.

The problem represents a systemic issue, not a personal failure on your part. You’re simply trying to ship a package, assuming you’ll be offered the best available options. But under the rules of the franchise markup, you're systematically steered towards the _only_ option available to that specific store, which is almost always priced higher than what a true multi-carrier comparison would reveal. We've seen it time and time again: customers paying 20-40% more than they should, simply because nobody puts the options side by side. Imagine buying groceries, but the store only sells one brand of milk, and that brand is 30% more expensive than what you know other brands cost. That’s the franchise markup in action for your packages. It’s a monopoly by omission, an exclusive club you're unwittingly paying dues to, package after package, week after week. And you absolutely deserve better.
