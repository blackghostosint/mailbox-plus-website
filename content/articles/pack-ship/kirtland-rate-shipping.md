## The Single-Rate Screen

The thing standing between you and a better price is not a scam. It is a screen that only shows one carrier.

Walk into a single-carrier storefront — the franchise shipping stores, the branded print-and-ship counters — and you are standing inside a contract. The carrier sets the retail rates the store must charge, and the store sells that one carrier's services because that is all the agreement covers. Ask "is there anything cheaper?" and the honest answer is a shrug: their system cannot display a rival carrier's rate. The cheaper option is not hidden. It is structurally invisible — deleted from the screen you are looking at before you ever walk in.

The difference between what you pay and the best available rate for that exact box — often 20 to 40 percent on a given shipment, depending on zone, weight, and service level — never appears on any receipt. It is just quietly gone.

Kirtland has the pattern. The nearest single-carrier franchise store is The UPS Store on S.O.M. Center Road in Willoughby — it will ship your package competently, and it will never show you what USPS or FedEx would charge for the same box. The nearest FedEx-owned counter is FedEx Office on Mentor Avenue in Mentor, and the same structural rule applies in reverse. So the choice Kirtland shippers actually face is: accept one carrier's rate, or become your own rate analyst across four carrier websites. Most weeks, the box just gets shipped.

## What It Actually Feels Like

Not choose which branded store to trust — choose the price. That is not a fantasy. It is just what happens at a counter that answers to its customers instead of to one carrier's franchise agreement.

## What You Lose by Staying With the Single-Rate Screen

**Money, on every box.** A markup you never see is still a markup you pay. Ship five or ten packages a week and it is not a rounding error — it is margin leaking out one label at a time, unrecoverable the moment it prints. Nobody sends you a bill for it. That is exactly the point.

**Options you were never shown.** The four carriers price different boxes differently — a service level that is overpriced for one package is the bargain for the next. A one-carrier counter makes that decision for you every single week. Over a year, you are not just paying more; you are shipping with one carrier's boxes and tiers instead of the right fit for each shipment.

**The repacking advice that lowers rates.** A counter loyal to one carrier sells you that carrier's boxes. An independent counter asks what you are shipping and puts it in whatever costs you less. That advice is worth more than any single shipment — and you cannot buy it at a counter that only sees one carrier.

Every week on the single-rate screen is a week of paying full price by default.
