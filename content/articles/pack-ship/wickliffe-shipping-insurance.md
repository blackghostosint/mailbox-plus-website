## The Hundred-Dollar Default

The thing standing between you and a real recovery isn't carelessness. It's a line in the carriers' terms of service that almost nobody reads until they need it.

FedEx states it plainly: the first $100 of value in your shipment is included in your shipping rate at no extra charge as part of their standard $100 limit of liability. USPS builds up to $100 of insurance into Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express, and Ground Advantage — but a plain First-Class Package carries no included insurance at all. UPS is the same story: declare nothing, and your maximum recovery is $100. These are liability _limits_, not promises to make you whole. The carriers are not hiding them — the pages are public. They're just written in the kind of language nobody reads at a shipping counter on a lunch break.

Here's the part that stings: declaring a value above the default is not insurance in the way most people hear the word. It's a claim on the carrier's liability ceiling, and it comes with rules — proof of value, packaging standards, exclusions. If the box wasn't packed to standard, the declared value you paid for can be worth nothing at claim time.

So the default covers almost nothing, and the upgrade covers almost nothing if the packing gives them an out. That is the system. It operates in plain sight, every day.

For most people, that sentence is where the recovery dies. You didn't know declaring was a separate step. You didn't know packing was part of the coverage. You thought the label was the promise. It never was.
