## The Franchise Markup

The mechanism taking your money is not a scam. It is a contract.

Most of the big national franchise counters operate as single-carrier storefronts. The corporate shipper sets the retail rates the franchisee must charge, and the franchisee sells one carrier's services because that is the only carrier the agreement covers. Walk in with a box and you get quoted that carrier's rate — not because it is the cheapest for your package, but because it is the only one on the screen.

A franchise counter cannot tell you "USPS Priority Mail would be $6 less for that box." Its own system does not show it. The cheaper option is not hidden somewhere hard to reach; it is structurally invisible. You are not choosing a rate. You are accepting the only one you were shown, and the difference — often 20 to 40 percent on a given shipment, depending on the zone, the weight, and the service level — is the markup you can pay for never seeing the comparison.

Eastlake makes this worse in its own way. There is no single-carrier franchise store in Eastlake itself. The nearest UPS-only store is on Som Center Road in Willoughby, about three miles southwest. The nearest FedEx-owned counter is on Euclid Avenue in Willoughby, on the other side of town. So the "option" Eastlake shippers actually have is: drive to one branded counter, accept its rate, and drive home — or sit at the kitchen table comparing carrier websites for half an hour per package. Either way, the comparison costs you time you do not have, so most weeks you skip it and pay.

## What It Actually Feels Like

You hand over the box. The clerk types, turns the screen around, and there is a number. Maybe you wince. Maybe you ask "is there anything cheaper?" and get a shrug and a "that's the rate."

You pay it because the alternative — opening four websites, re-entering the same dimensions into each one, second-guessing dimensional-weight math you never learned — is worth more than the difference. But it stings anyway, especially the weeks you ship five or six boxes and feel the same small sting five or six times. You are not wrong to feel taken. You were shown one price by a counter contractually unable to show you the others.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

You buy everything else by comparison. You compare gas prices across two streets. You compare a $40 item across three tabs before checkout. Shipping is one of the biggest recurring costs an online seller has, and it is the one purchase almost nobody comparison-shops — not because you are careless, but because no single place has ever shown you the whole board.

You should be able to walk in with a box, watch every major carrier's rate for that exact package appear side by side, and choose. Not choose which carrier's store to drive to. Choose the price. That is not a fantasy; it is what happens at any counter that answers to its customers instead of to one carrier's franchise agreement.

**Money, every single week.** If you ship ten packages a week, a markup you cannot see is not a small leak — it is a second rent payment quietly added to your cost of goods.
