## The Carrier Fine Print

The villain in this scene isn't some shadowy figure; it's a ubiquitous, legally enshrined mechanism known as "The Carrier Fine Print." This isn't just about small text in a contract; it's a fundamental flaw in the system that most people shipping valuable items from Chardon—or anywhere else—fail to fully grasp until it's too late. It’s the standard carrier liability limit, buried deep within the terms of service that every major shipping company provides. And it's designed, quite frankly, to reimburse you pennies on the dollar when a valuable item is lost or damaged in transit.

Let's dissect this villain. When you choose to ship with a major carrier – be it UPS, FedEx, USPS, or DHL – they all operate under a similar premise: they are transport companies, not insurance companies. Their primary business is moving packages from point A to point B, quickly and efficiently. To mitigate their own risk and keep their prices competitive for the vast majority of low-value shipments, they offer a very limited, standard "liability." For most domestic shipments, this default liability is a paltry $100.

One hundred dollars. Think about that for a moment. Whether you're sending a child's forgotten lunchbox or a rare antique from a Chardon estate sale, the default coverage is often the same. This isn’t an oversight; it’s a systemic, calculated decision. It’s a "blackout" period for your true investment, a franchise monopoly that dictates the terms of compensation, not by the true value of your item, but by a predetermined, minimal cap. It represents a vast, gaping canyon between what your item is genuinely worth and what the carrier is legally obligated to compensate you for.

The problem isn't your personal failure to read every single clause of a multi-page legal document. The problem is that this crucial detail—this $100 ceiling—is often obscured, downplayed, or simply assumed to be adequate until disaster strikes. The system is built on the assumption that most people won't ship anything worth more than that, or won't care enough to seek additional protection. But for you, shipping that exquisite Chardon maple syrup collection, or a cherished heirloom from your family home near Big Creek Park, or an expensive piece of equipment for your business, this assumption is devastating.
