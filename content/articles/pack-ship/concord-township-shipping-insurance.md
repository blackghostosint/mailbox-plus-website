## The $100 Cap

The mechanism working against you isn't a scam. It's a default, and defaults are powerful precisely because nobody reads them.

Every major carrier builds a standard limit of liability into its base rate. At FedEx, the first $100 of value is included in the shipping rate as part of their standard $100 limit of liability — anything above that requires you to declare the value and pay a fee. UPS states it plainly: if you don't declare the value at the time of the transaction, your maximum reimbursement is $100. USPS limits its built-in coverage on barcoded packages to $100 of maximum liability as well.

Three carriers, same number. That's not a coincidence — it's the industry default, and it assumes your shipment is ordinary. When yours isn't, the default quietly transfers the risk from the carrier to you.

And here's the part that stings later: even the $100 isn't automatic. Claims get denied for packaging the carrier judged insufficient, for items on the excluded list, for paperwork that missed a deadline. The cap isn't just low — it's a door that can close before you ever reach the number.

The reason it feels personal is that the claim process was never designed for you. It was designed to pay out the ordinary case and screen out everything else. You, the person with the genuinely valuable item, are the everything-else.
