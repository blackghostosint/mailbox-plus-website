## Willoughby Post Office Hours, Answered First

You searched for the hours because you need certainty out of your lunch break — a box has to go, and a guess about the counter schedule risks a wasted trip downtown. Here is the plain answer.

The Willoughby Post Office at 4040 Erie Street keeps its retail counter open Monday through Friday from 8:00 AM to 5:30 PM and on Saturday from 8:00 AM to 2:00 PM. It closes Sunday. A self-service kiosk in the lobby runs around the clock for stamps and postage, without a person behind it — and hours are USPS's to change, so check the posted hours before you go.

That is the whole schedule. ## The Single-Window Problem

Nobody chose this schedule badly. It is simply what happens when every mailing errand in a city negotiates its way through one accessible window. The post office sets its own hours — 8:00 AM to 5:30 PM on weekdays, 8:00 AM to 2:00 PM on Saturday, closed Sunday — and every resident of Willoughby, Willoughby Hills, and the neighborhoods toward the lake factors the adults' workweek into the same arithmetic. Saturday is the day most people are free, so Saturday is when the line happens. The window is hardest to use precisely when it is most needed.

Single-carrier retail counters share the structure from the other side. A counter that partners with a single carrier can only take that carrier's label — a different logo on the box means a different trip, and a different line to stand in. Neither arrangement fixes the Sunday absence either. So the errand you meant to finish becomes a routing puzzle: whose logo is on the label, which counter takes it, is that counter open when you are, and what happens if your answer is no.

The result is tape you are not proud of and a box you hold against your hip a little too tight — because somewhere in the back of your mind, the whole line is a room full of people who made the same Saturday plan you did. Not because the errand grew. Because the system never bothered to make room for the label you have.

## The Line With a Hard Stop

Inside the Saturday window, the queue moves at its own pace. The person ahead of you ships like it's a career. The clerk works carefully — that part deserves respect. But the closing hour does not extend one minute because you brought a box at 1:50, and a scan that arrives at 1:59 is a different Saturday than the one you planned.

You stand there doing arithmetic about cardboard. You worry the clerk will judge the tape job. You worry the label is crooked enough to get rejected. You worry you are the reason the line is not moving — and whether Monday's plan will survive the week with a box still on the floor of your car.

That gap — the size of the errand versus the size of the coordination it demands — is the whole frustration. Not shipping. The system's refusal to make a small task small.
