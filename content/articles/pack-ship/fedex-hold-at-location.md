## The Truck Rolls Through at 10:40

You are not home when the truck rolls through your street, and you already know how the next hour goes: the tracking page flips to Delivered, the driver is three houses away, and your package is sitting in plain view of the sidewalk for the rest of the workday. You cannot call the driver back. You cannot pause the truck. All you can do is watch the clock and hope you beat whoever else was watching.

A package that has not arrived yet is still FedEx's responsibility, and FedEx will absolutely change the delivery point mid-transit — to a staffed, secure location where the package waits behind a counter instead of on your porch. The option is called Hold at Location, it is free, and most people never use it because nobody shows them where the button is. Here is the button.

## Why Your Package Lands Where It Does

Your driver is not the problem — his scanner is. Hundreds of stops a day, and every package gets the same treatment: set down, photographed, next stop. The route is priced to maximize deliveries per hour, and nothing in that math pays him to hide your box. Porch placement is not a decision anyone made about your package specifically. It is what the stopwatch leaves behind.

Then the marketplace piles on. You order from a site that ships the moment you click buy — not the moment you actually need the item. The delivery date lands on a workday because workdays are when everyone orders. Nobody in that chain ever asks the one question that matters to you: where is this box going to sit, and for how long?

The result is a window. From the Delivered scan to the moment your car pulls in, your package is unattended, visible, and gone if someone else stops first. You did not choose that window. The mechanism built it, and it opens on every single residential delivery that does not require a signature.

## How It Works: The Redirect, Step by Step

You pull up the tracking number while the box is still moving — because once that truck hits your driveway, the button vanishes. Open the tracking page, find Manage Delivery, and walk through the door before it locks. If the shipped notification hit your inbox this morning, do it tonight. Not tomorrow.

**2. Choose Hold at Location and pick your spot.** FedEx shows you the pickup locations near the destination address: FedEx Office, Walgreens, select Dollar General, select grocery stores. Pick the one that fits your actual routine — on the commute, near the gym, next to the grocery run. If you make the request before midnight the day before the scheduled delivery, the package is usually available for pickup the next day.

**3. Pick it up with an ID — within 7 days.** You will get a text, email, or call when the package is ready. At the counter you hand over a government-issued photo ID and the tracking number, and whoever is working scans it and slides the box across. If your ID address does not match the label address, bring a second document with your name and that address — a utility bill, an insurance card, a property statement, or a credit card statement all work. FedEx holds the package for up to 7 days; after that it goes back to the shipper.

Miss the delivery? Signature required? Here is what changes.

- **Signature-required and adult-signature packages** are where the redirect earns its keep — you cannot authorize a porch drop for these anyway, and a hold at a staffed counter is the clean answer. Adult-signature packages are released only to someone 21 or older.
- **Already missed the delivery?** If a driver left a door tag, you can still pick the package up at the location listed — bring the door tag or tracking number plus the same ID. The pickup clock on a missed delivery can be shorter than the standard hold, so go by the date on the tag, not the 7-day rule.

## What You Give Up by Defaulting

**The package.** The obvious one. A visible box on an unattended porch is not a hypothetical risk — it is the single most predictable theft opportunity in residential delivery. The redirect costs you ninety seconds on a tracking page; replacing the contents costs you a claim, a wait, and in some cases a restocking fight with the seller.

**The 7 days.** This surprises people: if you request a hold, pick a location, and then forget to go, FedEx does not keep the package indefinitely. Seven days after it arrives at the location, it heads back to the shipper. A redirect you forget about turns into a return-to-sender, and now the refund or reshipment is a conversation instead of a click.

**The alternatives you did not price.** Redirecting to a pickup location is free. Rerouting the same package to a different residential address starts at $5.55 — and if it is more than 120 miles from the original destination, the published fees run from $14.50 to $33.50 per package depending on the delivery day. Same problem, different button, real money. People who never open the Manage Delivery menu pay for the other one.
