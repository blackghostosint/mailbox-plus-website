## The Scene That Starts Everything

You finally canceled your Spectrum internet. That was a twenty-minute phone call and a small relief. Then the confirmation email mentioned a return, and a figure you did not want to think about: an unreturned equipment fee.

Now the pile sits on your dining room table. An internet modem. The little power supply box with its cord. Maybe a router and a WiFi extender if you had them. And a question you have not answered yet: which of this actually has to go back, and where do you take it?

Somewhere, a clock is running. Spectrum does not charge a late fee for a slow return, but it does bill you for the equipment itself if it does not come back — $90 for a modem or router, $60 for a WiFi extender.

The hard part is not the drop-off. It is knowing what belongs to Spectrum and what does not — and for fiber customers, knowing what you are _not_ supposed to bring in at all.

## The Return Hiding in Fine Print

Spectrum's return rules are not cruel, but they are easy to get wrong, and each mistake has a price tag.

First, the fees. The unreturned equipment fee sits right in Spectrum's pricing disclosure: **$90 for a modem (wired or wireless), $90 for a wireless router, $60 for a Spectrum WiFi extender.** Return the gear after you cancel or downgrade, and the charge never appears. Miss it, and those amounts land on your final bill.

Second, the power supply. People forget this one constantly. The little box with the cord that powers your modem — that has to go back too. Spectrum says so explicitly: power supplies that came with your equipment must also be returned.

Third, the fiber gotcha. If you are a fiber customer, **do not return the SONU modem**. That white unit stays plugged in and operational at your home. Do not unplug it, do not bring it in — the wall-mounted optical gear stays put, just like AT&T's. And the fiber Universal Power Supply cord stays too. People who grab everything on the counter and haul it all down to the store end up returning equipment Spectrum did not want.

And fourth, knowing what belongs to you. The cables, the extenders, and the gear you bought yourself are yours. Only the rented and leased equipment has to come back.

## What It Actually Feels Like

It is the uncertainty that wears on you. You stand at the table and ask the same questions out loud: Do I take this to Spectrum or to a shipping store? Do I need a box? Do I have to return that little power box? Am I a fiber customer, and is that white thing supposed to stay in the wall?

Every wrong answer has a cost. Take the wrong box in and the clerk shrugs. Leave the right one out and the $90 fee shows up on a bill for an account you already closed. Return the fiber modem you were supposed to leave behind and you have created a problem that did not exist.

So the pile sits there for a week, then two. It is not that the equipment is heavy — it is the paperwork, the unknown, the feeling that you are one misstep away from being charged for the privilege of returning something you do not want.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

You rented a box from a company to receive the internet you paid for. When the service ends, you should hand the box back and be done. That is a fair, simple transaction.

It should not require decoding a return policy to avoid being billed for equipment you are happy to return. It should not hinge on a quiet detail like the power supply, or a fiber modem you were never told to leave alone. A modem you no longer use should never become a line item on your final bill.

## How the Spectrum Return Actually Works

Here is the whole thing, stripped down, from Spectrum's own published process.

**Step one — gather the right gear.** The modem, the router, the WiFi extender, and the power supplies that came with them. If you are a fiber customer, leave the SONU modem plugged in and in place — do not return it. Check what your account actually rented; that is what has to come back.

**Step two — pick your drop-off.** You have two good options:

- **A Spectrum Store.** Bring the equipment in and you get a receipt right there detailing what you returned and the date. You can book a store appointment online to skip the line.
- **The UPS Store.** Tell the associate you are returning Spectrum equipment. They package and ship it to Spectrum for you — you just hand over the equipment. Keep the receipt to track the return.

**Step three — the home kit, if you need it.** If you cannot get to a Spectrum Store or The UPS Store, contact Spectrum and request a **home shipment return kit** — a cardboard box with a return label and bubble wrap. Note the exceptions: Spectrum Apple TV and Spectrum Mobile devices cannot be returned in a home kit, and Multi-AP WiFi extenders need a specialized kit.

**One warning about Apple TV.** If you are returning a Spectrum Apple TV device, it is only eligible for a refund within 14 days of purchase, through The UPS Store, in its original packaging and same condition. Removed shrink wrap disqualifies it.

**And if your account is already disconnected**, contact Spectrum first before you drop anything off. Spectrum's own page points disconnected customers to customer service for help completing the return.

That is the entire procedure. Ten minutes of fear, one trip.
