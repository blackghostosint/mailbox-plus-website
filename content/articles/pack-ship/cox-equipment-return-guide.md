## Return Cox equipment at a Spectrum retail store or UPS Store

To return Cox residential internet or TV equipment, contact Cox to disconnect or downgrade service; then take the items in your service-change email to a Spectrum retail store or UPS Store. Cox says return it within **15 calendar days**; drop-off needs no box or shipping label.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

Your only real choice is which Cox-listed drop-off matches your situation: a Spectrum retail store or a UPS Store. Use the email from Cox to confirm the exact equipment on your account, make the service change before handing anything over, and keep the return record the location gives you.

## The Cox-to-Spectrum name mismatch is the trap

Cox's return page sends you to a Spectrum retail-store finder for a Cox equipment return. That name mismatch is easy to miss when you search for a Cox store, especially when a box and its cords are already waiting by the door. Use the destination labels and instructions on Cox's live page, not an old memory of what a store used to be called. If the choices change, the current Cox page and your account email take precedence over this guide.

## The deadline is real; the item list is account-specific

The modem sits on the table, with its power cord coiled beside it and a remote close enough to make you wonder whether it belongs in the return bag. The Cox email is open in front of you; your eyes keep coming back to the 15-calendar-day deadline. Cox's current residential instructions say equipment must be returned within that window to avoid unreturned-equipment charges, and in the same condition it was received.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

Do not assume that every device in the house belongs in the return. Cox says the email sent after a service change lists the equipment that needs to be returned. Its published list includes digital receivers, internet modems, telephone modems, remote controls, power cords and power supplies, and a Cox-supplied Netgear router for Gigablast customers. Use that email to decide what goes in the return—not a guess based on which cables happen to fit.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

If the message names a device you cannot find, or you cannot tell whether a cord belongs with it, ask Cox before the return. Sending the wrong item does not resolve a missing item on the account, and leaving behind something the email lists can create a second round of calls later. Your account's return list is the useful checklist; the general equipment list is only a starting point.

## Which Cox return option should you choose?

Cox lists free drop-offs for leased residential gear at a Spectrum retail store or a UPS Store. No box. No label. Use the live location finders before you drive.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

You do not need to know Cox's model names or explain why the remote is still on the table. The account email is the list Cox says to use. For leased residential equipment after a service change, choose either destination Cox names. Purchased equipment being returned for a refund follows a different rule: Cox directs that return to a Cox-owned retail store. Cox Mobile is different again and requires a label from an agent before a UPS drop-off.

## The concession: the retail store wins for a purchased-device refund

A UPS Store may be the more convenient Cox-listed stop for returning leased internet or TV equipment, but that does not make it the right choice in every situation. If you bought the equipment and are seeking a refund, Cox says to take it to a Cox-owned retail store within 30 days of purchase; its page says the refund is made by the method used for purchase. In that case, choose the Cox-owned store rather than treating the UPS Store as an interchangeable option.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

That distinction is easy to miss when a search result says “return Cox equipment” without separating rented equipment from purchased equipment. For rented equipment after a service change, use the drop-off option Cox lists. For a purchased-item refund, choose the Cox-owned retail store. Read the part of the page that matches your situation rather than assuming the routes are interchangeable.

The same caution applies to Cox Mobile. The Cox page says not to take a mobile device to a UPS Store without a return label provided by an agent; it tells customers to contact Cox to get that label. Do not use the residential modem-and-receiver steps as a substitute for the mobile-device process.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

Disconnect first. Cox says to contact it before returning equipment to avoid potential charges.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

At the drop-off, do not bring a box or print a label just because you are returning a modem. Cox's instructions say those are not needed when dropping off equipment at a return location, and that the listed drop-off methods have no fee. The page also says equipment should be in the same condition it was received. If a piece is damaged, missing, or not clearly listed, check with Cox rather than assuming that a routine handoff settles the question.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

## Make the handoff, then close the loop

A modem and a cord on the table can keep the task in your line of sight. Cox's instructions make the sequence plain: contact Cox about the downgrade or disconnect, use the account email to identify the items, and return them to a Spectrum retail store or UPS Store within 15 calendar days. Keep the handoff confirmation.[Cox's current equipment-return instructions](https://www.cox.com/residential/support/returning-your-equipment.html)

Then check your Cox account or bill to make sure the return has been recorded. If an unreturned-equipment charge appears even though you handed the equipment over, use the record from the drop-off and contact Cox about the account. Do not assume that a box being out of the house means the account has already caught up.

Don't apologize for not knowing what to return. Use Cox's email; it names the equipment for your account. Take that gear to a Spectrum retail store or UPS Store, keep the handoff confirmation, and check your account after the return. Once the listed items are in the bag, the table is clear; the email and confirmation give you something to refer back to if the account has not updated yet.
