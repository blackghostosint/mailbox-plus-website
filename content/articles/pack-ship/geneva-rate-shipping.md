## The Single-Carrier Screen

Call the mechanism what it is: the Single-Carrier Screen. Not a scam — a business model, operating in plain daylight.

A franchise shipping store signs its contract with one carrier, and that contract decides what the screen behind the counter is allowed to quote. What it quotes is that carrier's retail rate. If a different carrier would move your box to the same address for noticeably less, that number simply does not exist inside the store. Nobody lied to you. Nobody had to. The cheaper rate just never got printed.

And the rate on the screen isn't the floor, either. Because the store sells only that one carrier's published retail rate — the highest price that carrier lists — there is no incentive and no mechanism to hunt down a better number for your box. You're not just paying the wrong carrier's price. You're paying whatever that store decided to add on top of it, and you have no way of knowing how much that is.

The whole system runs on one assumption: that you will not comparison-shop. And you won't — not because you're lazy, but because comparing means driving the same box to three different counters and asking three different clerks to quote the same shipment. Nobody does that. The model was built counting on it.

## What It Actually Feels Like

It feels like a small doubt you swallow. The clerk turns the screen around, there's a number, you pay it. Somewhere on the drive home it hits you: was that good? You honestly don't know. There was nothing on that screen to measure it against.

Multiply that by every box you've ever shipped. Birthday packages to your daughter in Columbus. The grapevines and maple syrup you mailed to family out of state. The resale order you packed at your kitchen table. Every one of them closed with the same shrug — "I guess that's what it costs."

It isn't what it costs. It's what one store, wired to one carrier, decided to charge you.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

You should be able to see every carrier's rate for your package and pick the best one. That is not a radical demand. The carriers publish their rates. The box gets weighed on the same scale either way. The comparison takes seconds on a computer that is already sitting behind the counter. The only thing missing is the will to show you.

Transparency shouldn't be an upgrade you pay for. The person shipping a five-pound box to Cleveland and the person shipping a thirty-pound box to Phoenix both deserve to see the whole board before paying a dime.
