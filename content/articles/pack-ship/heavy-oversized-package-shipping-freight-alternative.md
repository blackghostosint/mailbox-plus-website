## The Surcharge Stack

The problem here is not one fee. It is a stack of them, each one legal, each one disclosed — in a service guide most people never open.

The first layer is **additional handling**. Both major carriers charge it when a package gets physically awkward. UPS applies it when the longest side exceeds 48 inches, the second-longest side exceeds 30 inches, the package weighs more than 50 pounds, or the box is over a certain cubic size. FedEx applies its own version on the same logic. Your box did not change. It just tripped a rule.

The second layer is the **large package surcharge**. UPS calls a package "large" when length plus girth exceeds 130 inches, or length exceeds 96 inches, or it weighs more than 110 pounds or takes up more than 17,280 cubic inches — and once it qualifies, the carrier bills a minimum billable weight of 90 pounds even if your box physically weighs less. FedEx runs the same play: an oversize charge past 96 inches in length or 130 inches in length-plus-girth, with the same 90-pound minimum. Read that again: a big, lightweight item — a rolled rug, a foam pad, a disassembled crib — can bill as if it weighed 90 pounds.

The third layer is **dimensional weight**, the one that bites everything. Carriers price space, not just heft. Multiply length by width by height, divide by the carrier's divisor — 139 on daily rates, 166 at retail — and if that number beats the actual weight, you pay the bigger one. A four-cubic-foot box of packing peanuts and one cast-iron skillet bills as the peanuts.

Stack all three and the quote you saw online — which assumed a tidy 20-pound, 18-inch cube — is a different product from the box on your garage floor.
