## The Sign-for-It Rule

Here is the thing almost nobody explains before you hit "checkout": **wine and liquor cannot be left at your door.**

When a carrier ships alcohol, both FedEx and UPS require what's called an **Adult Signature Required** delivery. It's not optional and it's not the driver being difficult. It's the rule.

- **FedEx:** Alcohol shipments require an adult signature. The recipient must be **at least 21 years old**, present a **government-issued photo ID**, and **sign in person** before the driver hands over the box. FedEx lists alcohol as a shipment type that _always_ requires a signature, no matter what the shipper picks.
- **UPS:** Same requirement. Wine, beer, and spirits move under **Delivery Confirmation Adult Signature Required** — the signature of an adult **21 or older** at delivery.

So no, the driver can't drop it behind the bush, can't hand it to a neighbor, can't leave it with a minor, and can't set it by the garage and mark it delivered. If no qualifying adult is standing there with an ID, the delivery simply **does not happen**. The truck drives away with your wine.

## What It Actually Feels Like

The first thing you feel is inconvenience. You rearrange your whole afternoon around a window the carrier won't commit to. Maybe you WFH on delivery day and hover by the front window. Maybe you text your neighbor to watch for the truck — and then remember they can't sign for it either, because they're not 21 on paper for a bottle of Cabernet that a stranger is about to hand them.

The second thing is worry. That truck is warm. It's late summer in northeast Ohio — inside a delivery vehicle, temperatures climb well past anything you'd want a bottle of wine sitting in. Every hour your delivery sits in that box is an hour your wine cooks. And the longer it takes to get to your door, the more of that $200 you start to feel like you wasted.

The third thing is the real one: **you miss the delivery, and you lose the day.** Redelivery happens in the same narrow window. So you're not solving a one-day problem — you're scheduling your life around a delivery that will only land on a day you happen to be home, at the exact moment a 21-plus adult happens to be standing at the door with ID in hand.
