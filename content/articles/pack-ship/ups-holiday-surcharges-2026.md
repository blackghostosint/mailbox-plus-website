## The Line Item You Never Priced

You ship the same boxes every week, and you know what they cost. Then October arrives, the label prints, and the price is higher — same box, same weight, same zip code, plus a line you never agreed to: **Peak Surcharge — Additional Handling: $11.90.** Nothing about your package changed. The calendar did.

It should not work that way. You should be able to know what a shipment costs before you promise a customer a price, all the way through the holidays. The fix is a counter that compares all four carriers against the current surcharge tables — not just the one carrier a franchise store sells. Here is how it works.

The mechanism has a name. UPS publishes it every fall in a pricing table almost nobody reads, and the fees start earlier than you think.

## The Calendar Tax

You probably missed the late-August announcement. UPS, FedEx, and the U.S. Postal Service all slipped new peak fees into their published tables this year — and UPS's are already on your shipments. These are not a rumor and not a billing mistake. They are a published pricing table.

Here is the 2026-2027 schedule, straight from UPS's published rate tables:

| Surcharge                      | Services affected                                       | Peak range per package | Effective dates                                       |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Additional Handling            | U.S. domestic and many U.S. export shipments            | $8.75 – $11.90         | Sept. 27 – Jan. 16, 2027                              |
| Large Package Surcharge        | U.S. domestic and many U.S. export shipments            | $96.25 – $117.50       | Sept. 27 – Jan. 16, 2027                              |
| Over Maximum Limits            | U.S. domestic and many U.S. export shipments            | $530 – $590            | Sept. 27 – Jan. 16, 2027                              |
| Demand Surcharge               | UPS Ground Residential, Air, and Ground Saver           | $0.50 – $2.50          | Oct. 25 – Jan. 16, 2027                               |
| Demand Surcharge (high-volume) | Qualifying customers billed over 20,000 packages weekly | $0.50 – $9.35          | Oct. 25 – Jan. 16, 2027 (higher-volume shipper table) |
| Surge Emergency Fee            | U.S. ↔ Canada and Mexico                                | $0.50                  | Sept. 27 – Jan. 16, 2027                              |
| Surge Emergency Fee            | UPS Worldwide Economy DDP and DDU from the U.S.         | $0.50 per pound        | Sept. 27 – Jan. 16, 2027                              |

Three things matter in that table.

First, **the fees start September 27** — weeks before most people think about holiday shipping. The handling, large-package, and over-maximum fees are already on. The per-package demand surcharge on Ground Residential, Air, and Ground Saver does not kick in until October 25.

Second, **the most expensive weeks run November 22 through December 26** — exactly the weeks you actually need to ship. That is when the additional handling fee hits its $11.90 ceiling and the large-package fee touches $117.50.

Third, **the fees run through January 16, 2027** — past Christmas, past New Year's, right when you are shipping the last stragglers and the returns.

And here is the part that makes the table personal: UPS updates this table every year, and shippers should check [ups.com/peaksurcharge](https://www.ups.com/us/en/support/shipping-support/shipping-costs-rates) for changes before every shipment — because the amounts, the windows, and the volume thresholds can all move.

The demand fee is also charged **on top of** the standard surcharge, not instead of it. If your box already trips UPS's ordinary Additional Handling fee — heavy, long, or oddly shaped — you pay the regular fee and the demand fee. Same for large packages. And the weekly fuel surcharge is still riding on top of all of it. That is why the same box can cost dramatically more in October than it did in August, without a single thing about it changing.

## What It Actually Feels Like

It feels like the squeak of the tape gun as you seal the same box you shipped for $14 in August — then watching the screen flash a number twenty dollars higher when the clerk spins it around.

You stand at the counter with the label in your hand and the number on the screen, and you have no leverage. There is nothing to negotiate and nothing to shop around for at the store you are standing in — because a single-carrier store only sells one carrier's surcharges. If you ask why it costs more than it did in August, the answer is a shrug and the words "peak season," as though the calendar itself were a natural disaster.

For online sellers it is worse. You did not price your products in July assuming an $11.90 surprise on every box that needs extra handling, or a $117.50 charge on the oversized item you sell every week. Your margins were already thin. The surcharge is not a rounding error — it is a business expense that appears exactly when you can least afford it, and you are the one absorbing it, because raising prices in December makes you uncompetitive in the one month you cannot afford to be uncompetitive.

And the fee is never itemized in a way you can act on. It shows up inside the total, buried in the fine print of an invoice, with no explanation and no warning.

## Why It Should Not Be This Way

A surcharge is a price increase that does not look like one. Base rates went up in January. The fuel percentage climbs weekly on top of that. And now the calendar adds more on top of the fuel. At no point in that chain does anyone sit down with you and show you the whole stack. At no point does anyone own the number.

A hundred-and-seventeen-dollar fee on a box you can carry with one arm should be a headline, not a footnote. A shipping price should mean something — you should be able to promise a customer a delivery date and a cost, and keep both.

You should not need a degree in logistics to ship a holiday gift. That is not a niche skill. That is the entire job of the shipping industry, and somewhere along the way the industry started charging extra for the privilege of understanding it.
