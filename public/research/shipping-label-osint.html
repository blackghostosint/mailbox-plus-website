The Label That Opens Every Door

A Guide for Anyone Who Ships or Receives Mail

Name ⟶ Phone ⟶ Email ⟶ Social ⟶ Profile

A shipping label seems simple. It has a name. It has an address. That is all.

That is enough.

A bad actor starts with two facts. Your name. Your street. From there, they can find your phone. They can find your email. They can find your relatives. They can find your Facebook, your Instagram, your LinkedIn, your Twitter. They can find where you work. They can find what your house is worth. They can find old addresses from five years ago. They can find photos of your face.

This guide shows how they do it.

It does not tell you how to do it to someone else. It tells you what others can do to you.

01 You Ship a Package

You tape the label. You drop the box in the mail. The label goes with the package.

The label shows your name. It shows your address. You meant no harm. You thought it was just paperwork.

02 The Problem

A stranger finds that label. Or a worker at the shipping hub photographs it. Or the stranger buys the data from a broker who scraped it from returns.

Now the stranger has a starting point. Name plus address is a skeleton key. One person searches one website. A profile opens.

03 This Guide

This guide is your window. It shows the path from label to full profile. You need to see the path to block it.

The steps are real. The tools are real. The results are real. Knowing the steps lets you stop them before they start.

04 The Seven Phases

1

Phase One. The People Search Engines

The stranger types your name and city into a website. These sites are free. They require no account.

  • TruePeopleSearch
  • FastPeopleSearch
  • FamilyTreeNow
  • Whitepages
  • Radaris
  • Spokeo

These sites pull public records. Utility bills. Property deeds. Court filings. Voter rolls. Phone books. They combine them into one page.

One name. One address. The site returns a list of phone numbers. It returns email addresses. It returns names of relatives. It returns old addresses. It returns associates.

Each new fact opens a new door.

2

Phase Two. Property and Public Records

The stranger clicks on the county assessor's website. They search your street address.

The assessor's page shows who owns the home. It shows the assessed value. It shows the last sale price. It shows the mortgage company. It shows the parcel map. It shows the lot size.

If a business runs from that address, the stranger searches the Secretary of State business registry. They find the LLC filing. They see the registered agent. They see the officers.

3

Phase Three. Phone Number Analysis

The stranger has a phone number from Phase One. They enter it into new tools.

PhoneInfoga shows the carrier. It shows whether the line is mobile or landline. It shows the country.

Truecaller shows the name linked to the number. It shows the location.

Spy Dialer plays the voicemail greeting if one exists. A voice confirms the identity.

The stranger adds the number to WhatsApp. A profile appears. A picture. A status text. Last seen time.

They check Telegram. A username. A bio. A list of groups.

They check Facebook. They use the Forgot Password page. If the number is linked, a partial name appears.

4

Phase Four. Email Analysis

The stranger has an email from Phase One. This is the most powerful key.

They enter the email into Epieos. The tool returns the Google account linked to it. It shows a profile picture. It shows Google Maps reviews written by that person. It shows a Skype account. It shows a Gravatar image.

It returns the email naming pattern for the person's employer. The domain is known. First initial last name at company dot com.

They enter the email into HaveIBeenPwned. The site tells them if the email appeared in a data breach. It lists which breach. It lists what was stolen. Passwords. Names. Addresses. Phone numbers.

They enter the email into the Forgot Password page for Facebook. Instagram. LinkedIn. Twitter. TikTok. Snapchat. Each site responds. Some show the full account name. Some show the profile photo. Some show part of a username.

5

Phase Five. Social Media Cross-Reference

The stranger now has a name. An address. A phone. An email. They search social platforms.

Facebook search: Name plus city. Results appear. The target's page.

LinkedIn search: Name plus employer. The person's work history shows. Current job. Past jobs. Coworkers.

Instagram: Name search. Or phone in the Find Friends tool.

Twitter: Name. Location. Advanced search with near:"City".

GitHub: Email or name. Code commits show the author's email.

Sherlock takes a username and hunts for it across four hundred sites. If the person uses the same handle everywhere, the stranger sees them all.

6

Phase Six. Advanced Methods

The stranger has a face from a profile photo. They upload it to Google Images. Yandex. TinEye. Baidu. Pimeyes. The search finds the same face on other sites. It finds other photos.

They search USPS Informed Delivery. The stranger signs up for the target's address using knowledge-based authentication questions. The answers came from the people-search sites. No alert goes to the target. The stranger now receives scanned images of the target's incoming mail every day.

They Google dork for documents. They type "First Last" "Street Address" filetype:pdf. They find court filings. Loan applications. Business records. The documents contain more data.

06 Other Defenses

Remove your data from people-search sites. The Privacy Rights Clearinghouse lists the sites. The BADBOOL list gives the opt-out links. Work through them. It takes time. It works.

Freeze your credit with Equifax. Experian. Innovis. TransUnion. This blocks knowledge-based authentication. It blocks the Informed Delivery trick.

Register for USPS Informed Delivery at your own address. Do it before a stranger does.

Use unique usernames on every site. Never reuse a handle. Sherlock cannot connect accounts if the names differ.

Check HaveIBeenPwned. See if your email appeared in a breach. Change passwords on affected accounts.

07 Avoid Failure

If you do nothing, the pipeline works. A name and address become a phone and email. Those become social accounts. Those become photos. Those become relatives. Those become a map of your life.

What inaction costs A stranger can watch your mail. They can see when you travel. They can see who writes to you. They can find your mother's name. They can find your children's names.

This is not fiction. This happens every day.
